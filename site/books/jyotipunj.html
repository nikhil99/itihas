Jyotipunjज्योतिपुंज · “a cluster of lights”

Narendra Modi's portraits of the people who shaped him in the RSS. Most of them were unknown outside Gujarat. The book's argument is that a large organisation grows from many small, selfless lives, the way a banyan tree grows from one small seed. This page shows who those people were, when they lived, where they came from and what they had in common.

The book in one picture

A banyan from a single seed

The introduction compares the RSS to the great banyan of Kolkata, whose branches put down roots until no one can find the original trunk. The book follows the same shape. Vivekananda's ideal leads to Hedgewar's seed in 1925. Guruji's 33 years form the trunk. The branches are the Gujarat workers, and the author stands at the top looking back.

Where they came from

Marathi-speakers who became Gujarati

Modi points this out himself: Anantrao Kale “was a Maratha but forgot all about his state” once he began working in Gujarat. Madhukarrao even banned Marathi in the Vadodara shakha so the Marathi-speaking volunteers would learn Gujarati. Here is each outsider's path into Gujarat.

What the book is really saying

Six ideas to take away

1 · Resolve matters more than resources

The RSS began with five boys and nothing to eat. Chipalunkar lived on bajra flour stirred into water while he started shakhas in Idar. Action is not wholly dependent on resources.

2 · Sanyasis dressed in white

Vivekananda turned the monk toward social service. Hedgewar then created the pracharak, a lifelong celibate worker who lives in society instead of a monastery. Swami Chinmayananda called them “Sanyasis adorned in the whites.”

3 · A Swayamsevak first and last

At a camp in Gwalior, guests were asked to leave the meal row first. Guruji stayed seated, because he was a Swayamsevak and not a guest. In this book, rank never outranks the volunteer.

4 · Careers given up

A London-trained pathologist, two engineers, a railway clerk, a mill hand, a goldsmith and a law student each traded a career for the work. The book treats these exchanges as the real cost of building the organisation.

5 · The organisation is a family

Nathabhai named his favourite households after their best dishes, such as the “dhokla family” and the “panipuri family”. Vakil Saheb hid a father's telegraph until a camp ended, then sent the boy home to his job interview.

6 · Tested by bans and the Emergency

The 1948 ban and the 1975–77 Emergency come up again and again. Pappaji, then nearly ninety, refused parole. Vanikar would not leave jail until democracy was restored. Vasantbhai kept writing his biography of Guruji while Modi carried messages between the underground and the outside world.

The author inside the book

Where Modi appears in his own book

Jyotipunj is partly a memoir. These are the moments where Modi steps into the frame.

Around age 20 · Madhukarrao Bhagwat Their first meeting. Modi felt Madhukarrao “infusing this energy into me” as part of a tradition, and says it was like temple bells ringing.
Early 1970s · Vasantbhai Gajendragadkar Modi became a pracharak in Ahmedabad and kept his distance from the senior leader until Vasantbhai scolded him for it. Later Modi lived in his home.
5 June 1973 · Guruji's death Vasantbhai heard the news from the morning paper and asked Modi, close to tears, why no one had wired him in the night.
1975–76 · The Emergency Modi kept the outside contacts for Vasantbhai, who was writing Sriguruji. Vasantbhai died on 16 February 1976. Their last joke was “Our vehicle runs in reverse.”
2 March 1981 · Keshavrao Deshmukh After a concert in Baroda for Guruji's birthday, the Prant Pracharak collapsed into Modi's lap and died. “I had seen death so near me for the first time in life.”
12 September 1988 · Anantrao Kale Modi wrote the farewell poem at the Valsad office when Kale went home to Undeel after 40 years in Gujarat.
Years of yatras · K.K. Shastri Shastri's wife, “Ba”, sent Modi besan laddoos wherever he was. After she died, Shastri handed him her last packet: “it is for the final time.”
As Chief Minister · Vasantrao Chipalunkar A day before Chipalunkar died, Modi brought him sweets to celebrate a Gujarat election win.
Source: Jyotipunj by Narendra Modi, English translation by A.K. Gandhi (Prabhat Prakashan, 2015). This guide summarises the book's own account. It is a hagiography written by an insider, so its portraits are admiring by design.
