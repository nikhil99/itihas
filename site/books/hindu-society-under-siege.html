Hindu Society Under Siege

by Sita Ram Goel · Voice of India · written 1981, later reissued

Goel argues that Hindu society is the one ancient society to have survived Islam, Christianity and Communism with its culture intact, and that its survival is no longer certain. He says the invasions were defeated but left behind “residues”: Islamism, Christianism and Macaulayism. Together with Communism, these form a united front around a divided Hindu society.

About this guide. This is a polemic. Goel says his terms refer to ideologies, not to India's Muslims and Christians, whom he calls “our own flesh and blood”. This page summarises his argument and attributes it to him. It leaves out his harsher language and does not endorse his claims.

Chapter 1 · significance of Hindu society

Invaded, but never absorbed

Goel rejects the picture of India as a country any invader could walk into. His version: early invaders were repelled or absorbed, the Islamic conquest met resistance that “did not cease for a day,” and Hindu society came out of each round with its identity intact.

Chapter 4 · the residue of Macaulayism

What the British found, according to Dharampal

Macaulay's 1830s plan was to make Indians “brown of skin but English in taste.” Goel cites Dharampal's study of British surveys in Bengal, Bombay and Madras. On eight counts, the indigenous schools compared well with England's at the time, even in decline.

“They scratched the soil… and the beautiful tree perished.”Gandhi at Chatham House, London, 20 October 1931, quoted by Goel

Goel names those he says escaped Macaulay's spell: Bankim Chandra, Vivekananda, Tilak, Gandhi, Malaviya, Savarkar and Golwalkar. For everyone else he calls Macaulayism “a creeping toxaemia,” a fashion that follows whatever is in vogue in the West.

Chapter 5 · the Communist conspiracy

Five targets Goel says Communism attacks

1 · Sanatana Dharma

The spiritual tradition of saints and bhaktas, called “sterile superstition”.

2 · Dharmashastras

The moral and social codes, reduced to caste oppression and Brahmin domination.

3 · Philosophies

Dismissed as a “Brahminical conspiracy” against the materialist Lokayata school.

4 · History

Gupta golden age called a myth. Pratap, Shivaji and Guru Gobind Singh recast as local rebels.

5 · Social institutions

Every custom traced back to a hidden class interest.

His main charge is about 1942. The Communists opposed the Quit India resolution after Hitler invaded the Soviet Union, and their theorists Adhikari and Ashraf made the case for Pakistan. Goel ranks the Communist contribution to Pakistan “next only to that of the Muslim League.”

Chapter 6 · the united front

Goel's conclusion

The front, he says, agrees only that Hindu society should disappear, not on what should replace it. He points to Zia's Pakistan and Khomeini's Iran as the Islamist model and to Afghanistan as the Communist one. Macaulayism serves as the “transmission belt” that makes the front's slogans respectable. His closing argument is that India's secular state and democracy depend on Hindu society surviving.

“India is secular because India is Hindu.”Goel, chapter 6
