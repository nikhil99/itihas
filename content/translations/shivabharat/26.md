# Canto 26

*The prince of poets said:*

1–4. Then, when the country was in terror and the merchants had fled; when Saswad had been plundered and Supe too, unhindered; when Pune had been occupied and Indapur taken; when the Chakravati country was mostly swallowed by the hosts of the enemy army; and when King Shiva, on the hill of Pranala, angry, was fighting there with the furious Johar — then, ah, the daughter of the Yadava king, Shah's wife, of great vows, filled with the zeal of the heroic mood, began to speak like a man.

5. That mother of King Shiva, living on Rajgiri, was devoted to guarding her own mountain forts.

6–7. Then the general Netaji, attended by many armies, having burned the whole of Shahpur and turned his back on the enemy, received Shiva's message as if it were a favour, and came with Hilal to Shivapattana.

8–9. Then he saw on Rajgiri the queen of King Shah, loving, long eager to see her child, ready to go herself to the hill of Pranala, given over to anger, and surrounded by her own great army.

10. As if he had done some wrong, frightened at every step, he bowed with Hilal to that lady of great vows.

11. Seeing those two bowing with folded hands, the fortunate lady said in a soft and deep voice:

*The queen mother said:*

12. "My son, my life outside my body, full of the zeal for war, is besieged on the summit of the hill of Pranala by his enemies.

13. "Ah! There you two have left your master, abandoned all shame, and turned back out of fear of the enemy!

14. "To free that child of mine, I myself will strive, and today I will take Johar's head in battle."

15. "Without him this quarter seems empty to me, like a forest. I shall bring back at once that child of mine, risen up like a lion.

16. "If for a moment I do not see that only son of mine, a portion of the one marked with the Shrivatsa, I shall give up my life.

17. "All of you, together, fight the enemy here. I myself will fight that enemy Johar."

18. As she spoke thus, the general, alarmed, said to that lady of fine conduct, who was deeply stirred:

*The general said:*

19. "By the power of the goddess, that great-souled, strong-armed man watches over the whole world; he does not need help.

20. "So, at his command, I went and conquered Vijayapattana, and I have come back — all good to you — to fight the copper-faced men.

21. "Let your ladyship, of firm resolve, stay to guard me here. There, the victorious Shiva is utterly fearless.

22. "When shall I see that lord of mine, the leader of the invincible? And when shall I join battle with the enemy?

23. "These many soldiers will fight the copper-faced men, and I will fight there with the enemy Johar and the others.

24. "The forts here, guarded with great care by Shiva's armies, the red-faced men cannot take now."

25. Having thus informed her there, the general valiantly set out for the hill of Pranala.

26. As he quickly mounted his horse, sword in hand and firm, armies of six kinds followed him, the jewel of a great line.

27. That strong-armed man led a great army, its banners grey with the dust shaken up by the trunks of the elephants.

28–29. Johar, hearing that an enemy army had come, guarded by the general and by Hilal, sent there many warriors of his own, bearing spears and broadswords, to oppose it.

30–31. Then all those Abyssinians, full of great pride, bearing spears, nooses, bows and arrows, bold in action, blocked in the middle of the road that very great and rich army of the lord of the mountain, raised their weapons, and fought it.

32. There battles took place between Hilal and the enemy, with heads cut off by arrows and hands cut off by spears.

33. Then Vahvah, the strong-armed son of Hilal, proud and angry, entered the enemy's array.

34. That youth with large red eyes, tearing open the chests of his enemies, was a sight to see as he showed the quickness of his hand.

35. Then, struck down from his horse by the great warriors fighting with many weapons, the proud youth fainted at the head of the battle.

36. Him, his lance broken, his body torn, his mind utterly confused, the enemies, greatly rejoicing, carried off to their own camp.

37. Not even Hilal could free that son of his, his very self, as it were, as the enemies led him away.

38. Johar's warriors fought there with such fury that Hilal and all the others fled in every direction.

39. Hearing that news, the general, the guardian of the rear, grieved in his heart for Hilal's heroic son.

40. In this way, though Netaji and the others fought day after day, they found no opening to get through to the hill of Pranala.

41. Hearing of the defeat of the general of the Bhosle king, Shiva grew angry with Johar, as the slayer of Jambha with Jambha.

42. Once, as that pure king lay asleep in a comfortable house, he saw in a dream Tulaja, the giver of boons.

43. As that very mighty man bowed before her, the lady of the world, very mighty herself, said to the lord of the earth:

*Tulaja said:*

44. "See, the city of Chakravati has been taken there by the copper-faced men. So you must not stay here; you must by all means set out, my son.

45. "Chakravati, caught in the jaws of the copper-faced men, will be the cause of great harm to them, like a hook to fish.

46. "Go quickly to Rajgiri, O king, and protect your own kingdom. Without you, your aged mother there is pining.

47. "Set out from here with only a few troops. Your warriors on the mountain will fight Johar.

48. "I shall bewilder Johar with my own power, and spread in the world the fame of your arm's victory.

49. "The days of this sinful man are numbered; he will not live long. Death wants to take him by another means."

50. Having instructed him thus, the goddess vanished there; and he, waking, bowed to her again and again.

51–55. Then, wishing to go out with a portion of his force from the hill of Pranala — which was ringed all around by the enemy army; lovely with peaks crowned by masses of clouds, its slopes thick on every side with dark forests, its cliffs echoing with the cries of peacocks, its many springs bursting from deep valleys — the lord of the Bhosles, intending to leave a strong and rich army there, put it in charge of the steady Brahmin named Tryambak Bhaskar, honoured among the people, after weighing him in his own mind; and spoke words, worthy and fitting, that condemned the hosts of the enemy:

*The king said:*

56. "See! Fighting here day and night with raised weapons, we mighty ones have passed four months, Vaishakha and the rest.

57. "This is the fifth month that has come, the auspicious Shravana, dark with clouds, spreading great beauty here over the forest-filled region.

58. "Ah! Johar, standing at the foot of this mountain, whom we of irresistible valour should have killed — he has not been killed by us. That is a wonder.

59. "Our generals, Netaji and the rest, have been broken, it is said; they cannot break this blockade of theirs.

60. "This strong Abyssinian, with his many strong-armed troops, cannot now be beaten by us; that is my judgement.

61. "While the Pune country is occupied by the copper-faced men, it is not right for me to stay here so long merely to restrain him.

62. "That great, bold army of the copper-faced men there will not, I think, be defeated by anyone but me.

63. "So I have set out quickly, wanting to kill the copper-faced men. Let this mountain, your shelter, be unassailable by the enemy.

64. "You be the general here, valiant as Senani, the war god, to destroy the enemies who have surrounded us on every side.

65. "Breaking through this array of the enemy and going on like Partha, no enemy warrior will stand against me; he will fall down."

66. Having said this and placed that steady Tryambak on that hill, the lord of the earth set out in the first watch of the night.

67. Ah! As their master went, seated in a litter, just six hundred foot soldiers followed him.

68. His drum for the march made a sweet sound, but the enemy thought it the rumbling of clouds and did not notice it.

69–70. Then the wind, heavy with the scent of kadamba, ketaki, kunda and kutaja blossoms, and slow from touching the spray of flashing springs, became favourable to him and showed him help as a kinsman would.

71. As the lord of the earth went along that path, which spies had seen, the lightning at once showed him the low places and the high.

72. Though he went close by them along that road, the enemies, bewildered by the goddess, did not know that lord of the earth.

73–77. Then the king, the lord of Rajgiri, crossed that road: rough with stones fallen from the mountain spreading here and there; very hard to pass because of rivers and streams roaring as they fell from the cliffs; thick with blue moss; with mud up to the knees; its great trees encircled by creepers glittering with fresh leaves; its caves full of tigers maddened by the rain; filled with peacocks moving about, crying in anger, wanting to kill the serpents coming out of their ant-hills; where no people were seen moving, a path seen only by spies.

78. Then, having crossed the road, he climbed his own mountain, Vishalgiri, broad as a pillar of the earth and fine to see, and made the desired stay there, a cause of rest for his army.

*Thus ends the twenty-sixth canto, called "The Entry into Svaragra", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
