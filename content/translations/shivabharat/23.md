# Canto 23

*The prince of poets said:*

1–2. Then King Shiva, on the summit of Pratapgad, looked at the enemy captains whom his soldiers had brought and heaped together before him: their arms bound tight behind their backs with their own turban-cloths, their faces drooping in the heat, bareheaded, necks bowed, minds dejected.

3–4. Then he placed in his treasury, which was like the house of Kubera, the gold and silver coins brought from the enemy army, the many heaps of jewels laid in chests, the pearl necklaces, and great loads of gold.

*The learned men said:*

5–6. When Shiva, with his army of foot soldiers, had drowned that army of Ali Shah in the ocean of the Jayavalli forest, where was his general with the cavalry meanwhile, and what did he do? Tell us, prince of poets.

*The prince of poets said:*

7–8. When Shiva, conqueror of enemies, set out for Jayavalli to kill by force the demon named Afzal, then, at his command, the general quickly set out, bound the enemy's lands, and broke his cities.

9–10. At this time the proud Muslim at Vairata, whose soul was set only on his master's business, sent captains of his army, of fierce valour, who were well disposed to him, and they began to attack Shiva's realm step by step.

11–12. The Yadava entered the Shurpa country, the Pandhare Shirval, Kharate Saswad, and Hilal the Pune country; and Hapsa and Saif Khan entered Tal-Konkan, thronged with enemy soldiers, and seized them.

13–14. Then Shiva's general, hearing of that suffering of the country caused by the enemies at Afzal's order — he who had set his heart on his master's business, glorious, devoted to battle, with seven thousand horse and with infantry —

15. turned back, the strong-armed man, wishing to conquer those strong-armed men, and went, swift as Garuda, to Shiva's country, which had been at peace.

16–17. "I shall make Kharate, Pandhare, the hard-to-conquer Yadava, Hilal and Saif Khan guests of Yama's house." Hearing this vow of his general, Shiva himself quickly sent him this message by his own envoy:

18. "The Muslim named Afzal, a demon hard to overcome, is coming to this Jayavalli with his army, wishing to make peace.

19. "So you must not fight him and his soldiers. Stay ready, but only until the terms of the treaty are settled.

20. "On the day of my meeting with him, on that very day you must without fail go to the neighbourhood of Vairata."

21. Ordered clearly in this way by King Shiva, he did not fight the enemy, and stayed where he was in between.

22. Then on the day of the battle between Shiva and Afzal, the general came to Vairata, on the day after.

23. Since he did not hold them back, Muse Khan and the other unconquerable Muslims, those enemies of the gods, fled in every direction.

*The learned men said:*

24–25. That climb, that descent, that road, that great forest; that opposition, that enclosure, that night, that defeat; that terror and that helplessness — how did they escape from around Jayavalli?

*The prince of poets said:*

26. When Afzal had been struck down in that battle, Shiva's foot soldiers surrounded the fleeing Muslims on every side.

27. Muse Khan, Hasan, Yakut and Ankush could not bear the great insult the enemy had done them there.

28. In the afternoon there was a great fight with each of them, terrible with great portents as if the sky itself were falling.

29. That hill country was good for foot soldiers but not for horsemen, so the Muslims, broken, sank in the ocean of fear.

30–35. Of one mind with each other, protecting one another, looking in all ten directions in fear of the enemy's approach, those enemies of the gods, leaving the battle, fled, abandoning their very tall elephants, their lovely horses, their boundless treasure and even their dear families. Wandering, tired, in the dense darkness, out of sight of the enemy, more and more frightened, every limb pierced by thorns, wet with blood, their strength wounded, their soles torn on the stones, intent on finding a way, they saw before them Pratapvarma, the kinsman of Chandraraj, terrified, his sight deceived, followed by two or three men, quickly running away, his face full of tears, wretched with hunger.

36. Then all those soldiers bearing weapons, their minds suddenly shaken, cried aloud, "This man must be killed!" and said to him:

37–38. "You who before, like a talebearer, came again and again, always talking falsely and hiding your own deceit — in the same way you brought Afzal, that great leader of the army, and at once made him, together with us, an offering in the mouth of the fire of Death."

39. When they spoke thus and raised their weapons to kill him, he folded his hands and bowed to them again and again.

40. Then Muse Khan, seeing him there pleading piteously, lowered his own voice and said to him with compassion:

*Muse Khan said:*

41. "You have seen this forest before, so thick and hard to pass. So go ahead of us and lead us out by some path.

42. "When we reach the Vairata country we shall do you good with love, as friends to a friend, to you who will have given us our lives."

43. Hearing these words from the mouth of Muse Khan, he, his mind greatly confused, agreed and promised.

44. Unseen by anyone, by a path he had seen before, he quickly led them out of Shiva's country.

45–46. In this way the son of Afzal, ashamed, took the elephants his father had first stationed at Vairata, with the equipment, the women's apartments, the treasury and some warriors, and, sick at heart, quickly left that place.

47. Though he pursued them at speed, the general Netaji did not catch sight of them as they quickly fled, and came back again to Vairata.

48–49. The strong and mighty Shiva too, eager to take the land quickly from the evil-minded Ali Shah, who hated him, filled the space between the quarters with the roar of his kettledrums for the march and reached Vairata with many armies.

50. Then, seeing that by his might the bank of the Krishna had been cleared of thorns, he rejoiced together with the delighted twice-born.

51. Then the valiant man made his general the leader of the advance, and attacked another country that the enemy had occupied.

52. Then his armies surrounded both forts, Chandan and Vandan, which were guarded by the enemy's army.

53. That king, the lord of Shri himself, with the fortune of a royal home, gained the city named Shrialaya, its enemies driven out merely by his name.

54–56. Then the two kings named Nayak, of the Yadava line, who after Afzal's destruction had lost heart and lost their support and had been driven out of the Pune country by the Bhosle armies, put forward the famous Hilal, the bought son of Khelkarna, gained the promise of safety, came for refuge to the great-souled one, and served Shiva, who was so very kind.

57. As those strong-armed men bowed, bringing gifts, Shiva, treating them as his own, made them prosper with riches.

58–61. Then the strong hero attacked Khatav, the deceitful Mayani, Rampur, Kaledhon, Valve, Haljayanti, Ashti, Ashta, Vadgaon, Velapur, Audumbar, Masur, Karhad, Supe, Tambe, Pali, Nerle, Kameri, Vishrampur, Savai, Uran, Kole and Karvirpur (Kolhapur), took much tribute from the strong, granted them safety, and brought those places under his rule.

62. Then the lord of the earth stationed his army all around in proper order and suddenly surrounded the hill of Pranala (Panhala).

63. Seeing the hill surrounded, the men who lived in the fort quickly lined the rampart and roared like clouds.

64. They showered the enemy with countless weapons, huge stones, great rocks and showers of fire-arrows.

65. The sky became covered as if with clouds by the smoke that rose from the mouths of the cannon.

66. In the battle there was a wonderful show of beauty, with showers of balls of iron poured out from the bellies of the guns, red as the newly risen sun, and with the cries of rockets that were like lightning.

67. Fire-arrows thrown up by the hosts of Shiva's soldiers, crackling loudly like lightning, fell there in such a way that the enemy could not even imagine it.

68. Then Shiva's soldiers, though checked by all those weapon-bearing enemies, climbed into the middle of the hill with great strength.

69. Leaping up and attacking from every side, Shiva's soldiers, swift as Garuda, took the lives of the enemy, alas, with cruel swords and sharp arrows.

70. I think that the fort's garrison, struck down by the arrows Shiva's soldiers shot, gave back a heap of riches, as one returns a debt to its rightful owner.

71. The lord of the Sahya, having taken it suddenly by force from his enemies, climbed the hill of Pranala, equal to the Vindhya, and looked around him.

72. Shiva set his army, which was of one mind with him, on the summit of the hill of Pranala, and passed the whole of that night as if it were day.

73. Again and again he looked at the hill's beauty, beyond compare, rich with ramparts, houses, step-wells, lovely gardens and boundless lakes, and still looked at it again.

*Thus ends the twenty-third canto, called "The Taking of the Hill of Pranala", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda of Nidhivasa.*
