# Canto 5

*The prince of poets said:*

1–2. Then the sons of King Vithoji, Khelkarna and the rest, like the sons of Dhritarashtra against the steadfast Dharmaraja, vied with King Shah at every step and, in their rivalry as kinsmen, showed him hatred as if he were an enemy.

3–4. Taking refuge with Ambar, the Muslim leader who was the Nizam's minister and who by his own wit had brought the earth under control, a man worth taking refuge with, and longing always for fortune, they could not bear the strong-armed Shah, bright as the sun.

5–8. The wise King Shah, who understood the signs, saw that a split had opened in his own family. Despising Ambar, the Nizam, and those wicked kinsmen working deceit against him, the proud man — full of zeal, valiant as Garuda, a home of victory, a spear on his shoulder — quickly went off by his own strength to his own estate with his army and all his war equipment.

9. Once he had left and settled with power in his own country, the Nizam's minister could not bring him under control.

10. Without him, the Nizam's kingdom, though rich, did not shine, as the sky does not shine without the sun.

11–12. Then Adil Shah, of great mind, seeing that this was the moment to divide them, called the strong-armed, high-spirited, great-souled Shah to his help with great kings, and thought himself, rival of the Nizam Shah, now unassailable.

13. Then, having gained him, Adil Shah grew, destroying the armies of his enemies as a forest fire, having gained the wind, burns down forests.

14. By his own valour, burning like the sun, he conquered the great-armed, noble Ambar.

15. As a storm, moving swiftly, breaks a firmly rooted tree, so he broke the Nizam's arm.

16. Then Ibrahim, pleased with him for destroying his enemies, gave King Shah, I think, half of his own rank.

17–18. Mudhoji, lord of Phaltan, a man served by many people, very valiant, proud and tall as a mountain, was hostile to Ibrahim. With his war gear the king attacked him with force and utterly crushed his great power.

19. A man of fierce deeds, he conquered the people of Kerala and the people of Karnataka too, and filled Ibrahim's treasury, pleasing him greatly.

20. He subdued other fierce kings as well by his own policy and made Ibrahim's kingdom like the kingdom of Rama.

21. As Gauri served the god whose banner is the bull, so Jija, born of the Yadava line, moon-faced and lovely-toothed, served the great king.

22. That virtuous queen, full of grace, with a face turned kindly towards him, fulfilled her husband's every wish.

23. Six sons with fine marks were born of her. Of them two, Shambhu and Shiva, made the line flourish.

24–25. Listen, best of the twice-born, as I tell how Shiva, Vishnu's energy come down to the circle of the earth, became the leader of all kings and the chastiser of hostile kings.

26. Long ago Kali, the age of Time, pleased the enemy of Tripura with fierce daily austerities and then, by obstructing the Vedas and shastras, grew strong.

27–28. Then demons — friendly to the wicked, hostile to the good, sinful, deceitful, enemies of gods and Brahmins — seized their chance and came down to earth in the form of Muslims, to overwhelm it.

29. By force those invincible men seized first the western quarter, then the northern, then the eastern and the southern.

30. Among them, who heeded only their own law, no sacrifices were performed; yet by the power of the Tishya age their fortune grew strongly.

31. Some kings they raised and set up, some they struck down in war. Through those strong men the warrior class mostly dwindled away.

32. Then the goddess Earth, weighed down by the burden of the Muslims, went for refuge to the lord of the worlds, the protector.

33. Pale with great torment, she bowed to the lotus-seated one, whom the thirty-three crores of gods worship.

34. Distressed, wishing to tell her many sorrows, she folded her hands and spoke to the supreme god.

*Earth said:*

35. "You are the father of all the worlds, and the keeping of the threefold Vedic dharma is dear to you. Lord of the worlds, why do you ignore me as I drown in an ocean of darkness?

36. "Viranchi, the world you made, moving and unmoving, is sinking, alas, because of demons with the bodies of Muslims.

37. "The very ones the gods once killed, the arrogant enemies of the thirty, have now in the Tishya age taken the form of Muslims and torment me.

38–39. "Krishna, destroyer of wicked demons, dwells in his own abode; the blessed one has taken the Buddha avatar and keeps silence. The wicked Muslims commit sins, and I find no protector. How shall I end this pain?

40–43. "The gods are not invoked, the fires receive no offerings, the Vedas are not studied, the twice-born are not honoured. Houses of free food do not run, nor do sacrificial rites; gifts are not given and vows are abandoned. The good suffer, all the bridges of dharma are broken, the Muslim religion grows, even cows are killed. Good people perish and the warriors are dwindling. A great fear of the Muslims has now come upon me.

44. "Now that I have fallen into the power of the Muslims, everyone mocks me, as the Veda is mocked when it falls into the mouths of fools.

45. "Whenever fear of the elder gods, the demons, arose for me in the past, you in your might always protected me."

46. Having said this to the lord of the worlds, the goddess of the earth fell silent, sighing, her eyes full of tears.

47. Seeing the Earth so troubled and unsteady, the Grandsire, home of the trust of all, comforted her thus:

*The Grandsire said:*

48. "Do not be afraid, timid one. All will soon be well with you, Vasundhara. Stay calm in your own place, and be steadier than steady.

49. "Long ago, for your sake, I prayed with the highest devotion to the enemy of Mura, ocean of compassion, and he himself said this to me:

*Vishnu said:*

50. "'Vidhi, do not worry. Listen to my words. What you want will soon come to pass.

51–53. "'Shah, son of Maloji, the best of kings, a great king of the South, of fine deeds and fine marks, swift as the wind, bright as the sun, conqueror of all, virtuous, of valour like Prithu's, is alive on the earth; and his wife, the great and virtuous Jija, daughter of the lord of the Yadavas, who furthers his victories, is wakeful on the earth.

54. "'That queen will bear me, made of radiance, in her womb. Becoming her son, I shall do what you desire.

55–57. "'I shall establish on earth the eternal bounds of dharma; I shall destroy the Muslims; I shall protect the gods; I shall set the sacrifices and other rites going again; I shall secure the welfare of cows and the life of the twice-born.' So promised the blessed lord, the all-pervading knower of past and future, and himself gave me leave to go to the world of truth, good lady."

58. Having said this and reassured the Earth, the Grandsire sent her away and returned to his own world.

59. Hearing those words of the creator, which brought the greatest good to the three worlds and delighted the heart, the Earth laid them up in her mind and waited for the moment she longed for. Throwing off the heavy fear that the Muslim demons had raised, she rejoiced, alas how fully, with the families of the brahmarshis, the gods and the twice-born.

60. Hari too, wishing to lift the heavy burden from the earth and at once to destroy the demons, enemies of the thirty, who had entered her in the form of Muslims, quickly resolved with compassion to take a matchless human form.

*Thus ends the fifth canto, "The Telling of Vishnu's Incarnation", in the Anupurana composed by the prince of poets.*
