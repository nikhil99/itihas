# Canto 30

*The prince of poets said:*

1–4. Then the strong king brought in with his armies the various Firangis — of matchless valour in aiming their cannon, skilled at fighting from ramparts, outdoing the lord of wealth in riches, masters of craft like the demon Maya, unconquerable in moving across the sea, following wrong paths, lower than the Muslims — and likewise the Malabaris, devoted to their ships and very rich, and the many sea-traders who had taken shelter on distant islands, and his vassals who had been unruly like maddened elephants; and he made them pay tribute.

5. He at once took into his own hand the wealth stored up in Rajapur, long guarded by those tight-fisted, greedy men.

6. That ender of the wicked had many diggers dig up the ground, whose belly was filled with pots full of buried gold.

7. Though the king's eyes had not been anointed with the magic salve of the adepts, still he saw the buried treasures there.

8. Wherever the king cast his eyes, there heaps of gold appeared, like Mount Meru.

9. With the many heaps of jewels offered by the great merchants there, a mountain of beryl seemed to stand right beside him.

10. Did he not, with his diggers, purify there the ground, together with its buried treasure, which had grown impure through long contact with the Muslims?

11–23. Gold and silver, brass and lead; copper, iron, tin, glass and pyrites; pearls, emeralds, rubies, diamonds and coral; rhinoceros horn, yak-tail whisks, ivory and vetiver; musk and saffron, sandalwood and camphor; black aloewood, kachura root, kankola and red sandalwood; cardamom and cloves, cinnamon and cassia leaves, deodar; the clearing-nut, nakha perfume, nalada and nagakesara; nutmeg, the datura, opium and cinnamon leaf; the rajadana fruit, kandarala, grapes and dates; dry dates, pepper, areca nut, devadaru and dry ginger; long pepper root, cardamom, chavya, turmeric and rock salt; black salt, potash, natron and myrobalan; frankincense, resin, bitumen and beeswax; lead ore and the peacock-necked collyrium, and antimony from the Yamuna; ajwain, asafoetida, cumin and lodhra; guggul, fire-crest, madder and red lead; mercury and orpiment, sulphur and realgar; lac, gum-resin, gorochana and mica; and all kinds of poisons and their antidotes; silks, linen, flax, cotton; and woollen garments of the ranku deer and of sheep, all new — these and many other goods besides the king then had carried by great men of burden, by horses, by oxen with their burdens, and by men carrying loads on their shoulders and on carrying-poles, and placed in his various forts.

24–25. Shethvali, Sondal, Harchiri, Nevare, Nandhvade, Kotvade, Kelvali, Kasheli, Pavas, Dhamanse, Belvade, Kharepatan and other old towns too brought him tribute.

26. Having thus brought those various districts under his control and collected many kinds of wealth, the valiant one turned towards his own realm.

*The learned men said:*

27–30. Having driven out the Muslim army, he quickly took Dalbhyapura; likewise he brought into his own hands the town of Chiplun; he took the town of Sangameshvar too without effort; ah, he dug up Rajapur down to the underworld; he seized all its hoards and took its townsmen prisoner; and by his might he made the lord of rivers, the sea, pay him tribute. When King Shiva, out of enmity, had overrun his realm in this way, what plan did Adil think of then?

*The prince of poets said:*

31. "As our enemy went towards Rajapur, you did not stop him on the forest road, so hard to pass."

32. "Let that be. But now that our arrogant enemy has turned back and come near you, block him."

33. So, grieving at heart, Adil sent his own command there to Suryarao, king of Shringarpur.

*The learned men said:*

34. Why then did Adil pass over those commanders of his army and appoint that king to this task?

*The prince of poets said:*

35–36. Thinking to himself, "Rustam and the others, already broken by Shiva, will not be able to fight him," Adil appointed him, they say, to this hard task, since he lived in the middle of the forest and had foot soldiers.

37. Then that mighty king, whose only wealth was his pride, at Adil Shah's command took up a mind full of enmity toward Shiva, as an elephant in rut takes up enmity with a lion.

38. He swiftly besieged with his own army the force that Shiva, skilled in deployment, had stationed at Sangameshvar to clear the hard road for travel.

39. With a wealth of foot soldiers he besieged Shiva's great army at midnight, and, eager to fight through ill fate, he roared loudly for a long time, like a cloud.

40. Tanaji and the others there could not bear his roaring, loud in their ears, any more than Garuda can bear the hissing of a snake.

41. Then, they say, Pilaji, son of King Nilakantha, struck down by fate and greatly afraid, thought flight from the battle better than giving glory to the lord of Prabhavali.

42. As he trembled and was greatly distressed, threw down the sword in his hand and ran, Malusare himself went a few steps quickly, they say, caught him by the hand and rebuked him.

*Malusare said:*

43. "I am your helper in this battle. Alas, you are leaving your own people and running away! Where have those boasts gone that you made before, commander?

44. "You whom the lord of the earth, Shiva, has favoured with greatness and protected — you, a commander, are running away today, abandoning your army! Alas, are you not ashamed?

45. Saying this, he at once tied the frightened man fast to a rock with pieces of rope, keeping him close beside him; and then, showing his own heroic power to the brave at every step, he danced there.

46. Shining in battle like an ornament in the ear of the lovely Glory of War, who scattered the floods of blood of the enemy heroes he struck down, that Tanaji Malusare blazed so with his splendour that there was a sun in that night.

47. Then his skilled soldiers, ready at every step and with gleaming swords in hand, eager to kill those who cried out their victory shouts swallowing the thunder of clouds, guarded him in that great clash with the enemy.

48. "Stand! Strike! Give! Stop! Push! Get them! Save! Leave it! Let go! Run! Lead! Turn back! Look! Split! Cut! Take! Bring down! Drop it!" — so they shouted aloud, and an uproar rose from the warriors of both sides as they roared and rushed at each other.

49. With five-branched hands, their nails glittering with the play of light from their shining jewelled rings; with heads, their turbans fallen, thickly strewn; with arms; with other limbs; with sword-blades that flashed for a moment and then were cut; with heaps of streaming blood from cut-off feet — that hill country became hard to pass.

50. Then, as the night came to its end, the enemy army, utterly broken by the fine warriors of King Shiva, at once came to an end, as darkness does before the rays of the sun.

51. Then, as those bulls among the hostile king's soldiers at once took to flight, Malusare roared together with his drum, which boomed like a new cloud.

*Thus ends the thirtieth canto in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
