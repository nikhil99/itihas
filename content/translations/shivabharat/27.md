# Canto 27

*The learned men said:*

1–3. When he had come out of the hill of Pranala, a fort made impregnable by the army he left there, with six hundred foot soldiers and unseen, how did Johar, bewildered by the goddess's yogic maya, come to know of it? And what counter-move did he make, surrounded by his own army? Paramananda, whose mind is steeped in the ocean of milk that is the hero Shiva's fame, tell us all of it.

*The prince of poets said:*

4. When Shiva himself wished to leave the hill of Pranala, he sent this message to the hero Johar:

*King Shiva said:*

5. "Called by Adil, the copper-faced men, provoked by my going beyond the bounds, have attacked my country.

6. "So I am setting out from here now to fight them. Alas, you must fight here with my warriors, skilled in war.

7. "Or, if you have a mind today for a duel with me, come and grace the foot of the hill of Pranala.

8. "Ah! With our own armies watching from a distance, the two of us, bearing swords, will fight there gladly."

9. Though he heard that message, the skilled lord of Karnapura acted as if he had not heard it, his mind alarmed.

10. From then on he loudly ordered his warriors, Bhai Khan and the rest, to blockade that mountain on every side.

11. "Not even an ant goes out or comes in unknown": so closely did Johar's soldiers sit around it there.

12. As if blinding those enemy fighters sitting there, Shiva by his own valour broke through that blockade.

13. Then, they say, in seven watches he covered a road measured at five yojanas and reached the mountain Vishala.

14. Then spies who knew the facts reported to Johar that King Shiva, son of King Shah, had left the hill of Pranala.

15. Then Johar himself, as if caught in the whirlpool of the ocean at the end of the world, was utterly bewildered and grew distraught in the midst of his council.

16. "Ah! How has this enemy, whom we had shut in on this mountain, suddenly come out today, leaving us astonished?

17. "Now how shall I show my face to Adil Shah? From now on my life will serve only to be laughed at.

18. "Hearing that the destroyer of the Muslims has slipped out of my hands, what will even Shaista Khan and the others say of me?"

19. After long regret and thinking it over again and again, he said to the arrogant hero, the Abyssinian named Masud:

*Johar said:*

20. "Now that this enemy has got free of the blockade, like a jewel slipping out of a knot, my mind is troubled indeed.

21. "Strong-armed Masud, follow him quickly, who escaped today from the hands of all of us as we watched.

22. "Absorbed in other business of his own, he will not, alas, stay long on Vishalgiri. So make haste."

23. Ordered by him in this way, Masud, outstanding in heroic deeds, ran after that enemy with a great army.

24. On the road his horses sank to the knees in the mud, and even the foot soldiers fell in the places thick with moss.

25. Hearing that Masud had come, the valiant King Shiva, on that mountain, readied himself loudly for battle.

26–28. Jasvant, the hero, lord of men and lord of Palivan; the valiant Suryarao, king of Shringarpur; and other vassals too, whom the evil-minded Johar had earlier appointed to blockade this mountain — though they fought again and again, beaten at every step, they could not stop Shiva from climbing to the top of the hill.

29. All of them, joined with the proud Masud and his army, again besieged that mountain, raising their weapons.

30–31. Then King Shiva's warriors, their eyes red with anger, came down from the mountain roaring like clouds, ran at the watchful besiegers who were attacking, and cut them down with streams of sharp swords.

32. There many Abyssinians, cut down by the edges of the swords of Shiva's strong foot soldiers, went to the city of Yama.

33. Jasvant, Suryarao and the many vassals could not hold out against them there.

34. Then the swift Masud, turning back his own fleeing army, attacked the enemy as one planet attacks another.

35. Then there was a great clash of the two armies, as they struck one another with swords and spears.

36. Then the enemies, masters of battle, laid low the Abyssinians, who came on like obstacles, maddened with pride in the strength of their arms.

37. As clouds with lightning break trees and as Garuda seizes serpents, the soldiers of the lord of the mountain, having conquered them, roared.

38. Then the field of battle was strewn with broken helmets, hands and feet, broken heads, shoulders and thighs.

39. The lowland beside Vishalgad, green with tender grass, at once grew red with the blood of the enemy heroes.

40. The earth became hard to look upon, with the thighs, knees, shanks and heads of men and horses.

41–42. So Masud, having drowned the whole of his own great army in the ocean of Shiva's wrath with his own hand, was put to shame; let go with his life by enemies who were masters of battle and did not care to kill him, he turned away from them in utter despair.

43. When he came fleeing from that battle and stood before him, Johar thought he had come back from the other world.

44. Then, gathering the army he had earlier brought and stationed on the borders of his own country, Shiva himself set out from the mountain Vishala.

45–49. Halting for five nights on the road, amid rejoicing, the king, lord of Rajgiri, quickly went to Rajgiri and bowed to his strong-armed mother: seated on a seat of jewels glittering with masses of rays, surrounded by women of good family, the household goddess of women of good family; keeping vow after vow and worshipping the gods night and day; giving him blessing after blessing, speaking true and deep words; her long eyes still with a flood of tears of joy; her love for her son overflowing; eager to see the good; bathing him in the nectar-like streams of milk that flowed from her breasts.

50. Then, telling her all that had happened, one thing after another, the victorious son of Shah spent the whole day near her.

51. When the victorious king came to his own Rajgiri, his kettledrums sounded with a gentle and deep voice.

52. Having thus at once defeated the Abyssinian army by the strength of his arm, when King Shiva came from the hill of Pranala to Shivapattana, then, though his armies were all with him there and anxious to make every effort, the matchless uncle of the lord of Delhi began to doubt, they say, whether he would gain what he wanted.

*Thus ends the twenty-seventh canto, called "The Entry into His Own City", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
