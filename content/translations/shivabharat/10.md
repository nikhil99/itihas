# Canto 10

*The prince of poets said:*

1–2. When Shiva reached his twelfth year, King Shah, at the command of the bearer of Pinaka, called the younger brother of Shambhu, Shiva, bright as the sun and of good marks, and made the far-seeing one lord of the Pune country.

*The learned men said:*

3–4. Prince of poets Paramananda, tell us how, at the command of the blessed god, the enemy of Tripura, the king sent Shiva to the Pune country, and how he reached that country at his father's order.

*The prince of poets said:*

5–10. Once, after worshipping the blessed god whose banner is the bull in his palace, the virtuous King Shah lay on a comfortable bed. In a dream he saw before him, smiling, the enemy of Tripura: his five faces serene, with ten arms and three eyes; lovely with matted locks moist with the waters of the Mandakini; the crescent of the cool-rayed moon on his head, his brow lovely with the triple mark; his throat like an emerald; adorned with serpents; giver of boons and of fearlessness; a hero bearing many weapons; wearing a leopard skin as upper garment and an elephant skin as lower; the source of every liberation, the treasure-house of every fortune; a yogi, lord of yogis, worshipped by Indra, Upendra and the rest; with all the worlds, and with the mountain's daughter.

11. Seeing the Lord himself before him, he bowed, and stood before him with folded hands, deeply content.

12. Then the blessed Bharga, whose power none can check, showed his favour to his devotee, the lord of the earth, with these words:

*The Lord said:*

13. "Strong-armed King Shah of the line of the Sun, great of mind, listen to my words, which are for your good.

14. "Know that this younger son of yours, who shines beside you, marked with good signs, is the Supreme Person.

15. "Your son Urukrama, growing step by step, will overrun the whole earth and kill the Muslims.

16. "This blessed goddess Girija, who loves her devotees, will come to him from time to time and protect him.

17. "Remover of the earth's burden, slayer of hostile kings, this devotee of mine will be unconquerable by everyone.

18. "So appoint this strong-armed, great-souled one named Shiva to the honoured lordship of the Pune country."

19. Having said this, the Lord himself put a string of pearls around the prince's neck.

20. When the god with matted locks had shown himself in this way, his heart full of love, the lord of the earth woke at the hour of Brahma.

21–22. Marvelling, his mind astonished at his own austerity, meditating again and again on that very form of the Lord, in the morning he called his family priest Prabhakara, bright as the sun, and told him his dream.

23. Then, with the approval of that delighted priest, Shah appointed Shiva to the lordship of the Pune country.

24. Given that lordship by his valiant father and eager to go to his own territory, King Shiva shone.

25–27. Then, on an auspicious day, King Shah sent his virtuous son to the Pune country with some elephants, horses and foot soldiers; with hereditary and trusted ministers and famous teachers; with titles, tall banners and a large treasury; and with attendants of unequalled deeds.

28. Then, in a few days, the scion of the Sun went from the Karnataka country to the land of Maharashtra.

29. With the three powers — of might, of energy and of counsel — and with his gathered armies and his own fortune, Shiva reached the holy city of Pune.

30. Friend of the chakra, who at once made the whole land glad, the people saw that friend of the world but did not know him for what he was.

*(The verse plays on the sun, friend of the chakravaka bird and of the world: Shiva, friend of the realm, came like the sun.)*

31. Then, with ministers in harmony with him and delighting his subjects, the valiant one grew step by step, and his fame grew with him.

32. Through his support, the country of Maharashtra became rich and full of people, and lived up to its name, "the great kingdom".

33. The teachers, humble and virtuous, who had approached him with devotion and with whom he was one in mind, found their purpose fulfilled.

34–40. He became expert in the Shruti, Smriti and Puranas, the Bharata and the science of government, in all the shastras, poetry and the Ramayana, in physical exercise, architecture, horoscopy and mathematics, in the science of the bow and of medicine, in physiognomy, in the various languages, metres and fine sayings; in riding elephants, horses and chariots and in their marks; in mounting, swimming, running and leaping; in the sword, bow, discus, lance, broadsword and spear; in battle and wrestling; in making forts impregnable and in storming them; in hitting targets hard to see; in reading gestures, in illusions, in drawing out poison and the like, in judging gems, in concentration, and in scripts. Thus the all-pervading one, who already knew them all, by mastering them himself, joined each of those teachers to great fame.

41. As the wish-granting tree of the gods shines in the splendour of spring, so he shone at the start of youth with fresh beauty.

42–43. To him, past boyhood and in the bloom of new youth, lovely with a beauty like the love-god's, came a wife, a virtuous and well-conducted woman of lovely form, shining with every virtue, born of the Pawar family.

44. Winning this fair-skinned woman, his love in a former birth, he rejoiced as Shri Krishna did in winning Rukmini.

45. Then, as that couple, familiar to each other from a former birth, came to love each other, the three aims of life — dharma, wealth and love — met in them and took on great splendour.

*Thus ends the tenth canto in the Samhita, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda of Nidhivasa.*
