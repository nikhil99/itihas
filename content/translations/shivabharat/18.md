# Canto 18

*The learned men said:*

1. Why did that wicked man pass by Shiva, who lived in the city of Pune, and go with his army to the Vairata country?

*The prince of poets said:*

2–3. Listen, wise men, as I tell you why the Muslim Afzal, hard to withstand as Kalayavana, setting out quickly at his master's command to conquer the proud Shiva, entered the Vairata country.

4–6. After killing in battle Bajaraj and the arrogant Krishnaraj, and their very mighty father Chandraraj, Shiva had taken the city called Jayavalli, a fort hard to take: reached by hidden paths, lying along the Sahya range, its country mostly forest, sheltered by a great fort, with wakeful soldiers in it, terrible with its rampart of mountains, and full of hosts of wild beasts.

7. Those who had been Chandraraj's helpers and kinsmen there, all of them Shiva, a hero and the best of the strong, cut down.

8. Then Chandraraj's kinsman named Pratapvarma, fleeing for fear of Shiva, went over to Adil.

9. Wanting Chandraraj's place, that schemer, surrounded by his counsellors, long pleased Adil with his service.

10–11. "I will take that country of Chandraraj, sheltered by great forests, from the king and give it to you without fail." Adil then accepted those words of his, and he gave help to Afzal's campaign.

12. Then that proud kinsman of Chandraraj told Afzal of the division there and brought him to Vairata.

13–14. "Whoever holds Jayavalli holds Vairata completely, and the whole of the Sahya range with the sea and its shore." Thinking this, and ready to take that first, the strong-armed Muslim quickly came to Vairata.

15–16. Then, when Afzal the Muslim had come with his army to Vairata and was eager to take Jayavalli quickly, King Shiva, skilled and intent on striking back, thought to himself as a lord would, very proudly:

17. "This proud man sent by Adil, who is angry with me, will show a manliness fit for himself, of manly valour.

18. "This is the Muslim through whose very great wickedness, ah, the might of this Kali age keeps growing.

19. "Through his wickedness the goddess Tulaja, who dwells in Tulajapur, was greatly insulted — he who has the energy of Nishumbha.

20. "He who is always merciless and wrathful, a heap of sin, seems to want to kill the twice-born as soon as they come into his sight.

21. "Like a mountain of sin, risen up on every side in pride, he has set himself to block the path of the dharma of the varnas.

22. "Forbidding all dharmas and promoting adharma, this man who must be killed has come near: I must kill him.

23–24. "The creator made cows on earth, with their milk and ghee, the materials of the offering, for the rites of those who extend sacrifices with due rule. Alas, that dark-souled man kills them day after day, and wants to overturn dharma entirely.

25. "This goddess Earth is surely held up by dharma; and that dharma is surely held up by the gods, and they by the twice-born.

26. "So the twice-born, the root of the whole world, must always be protected with care and honoured.

27. "To protect the gods, the gods on earth and the cows, I alone am born, again and again, age after age.

28. "I who entered the ocean and took the form of a fish killed the demon Shankha in battle and brought back the Vedas.

29. "I who became a tortoise and held Mandara on my back gave the earth its firm support.

30. "I who quickly lifted the earth from the ocean in the form of a boar killed Hiranyaksha.

31. "I, Hari, who became Hari the Dwarf, the younger brother of Hari, the very deceit itself, sent Bali to the underworld.

32. "I who appeared as a man-lion out of a pillar in the hall tore apart with my nails Hiranyakashipu, enemy of the gods.

33. "I who, as the jewel of Bhrigu's line, son of Renuka, killed Kartavirya in battle, made the earth empty of kshatriyas.

34. "I who became Dasharathi, built the bridge across the ocean and with a single arrow struck down the row of the ten-necked one's heads.

35. "I who, as Shauri, hero and jewel of the Vrishni line, established dharma, killed Kamsa and the other wicked ones.

36. "I am that Vishnu, the all-in-all of all the gods, the Lord, come to earth to take away this burden of the earth.

37. "These lines of the Muslims are all portions of the demons, risen up to drown the world in their own religion.

38. "So I shall kill these demons in the shape of Muslims, and I shall spread the fearless path of dharma.

39. "This fearsome forest of Jayavalli is my lion's den. Afzal, the enemy elephant who has come into it, will surely be destroyed.

40. "Like a moth flying up by the strength of its wings, not seeing its own end, he will meet me and surely die."

41–43. Having resolved this in his heart, Shiva, the Supreme Person, instructed his general to devastate the enemy's realm; appointed men suited to each task, devoted to his good, for the protection of his realm and his forts; and himself, skilled in the six measures of policy, crusher of enemy heroes, went to Jayavalli surrounded by his infantry.

44–46. Then Afzal heard that Shiva — whose intention was hidden, whose strength of arm was boundless, famous, unconquerable by others, possessed of the three powers — had himself taken his stand at Jayavalli, ready to fight, with his armies in close array. Listen, all you learned men, as I tell you exactly the message he sent to the all-knowing Shiva.

*Afzal said:*

47. "The insolence you show at every step pierces Adil's heart like a thorn.

48. "When the Nizam was ruined, Adil himself, wishing for peace, gave to the copper-faced men the land that had been the Nizam's.

49. "That country of theirs, sheltered by mountain forts, you, son of King Shah, have seized and taken over.

50. "The lord of Rajapuri, whose lands you, constantly winning at every step, took one by one, is angry and besieged.

51. "You attacked Chandraraj's great kingdom, so hard for others to assail, and seized it by force with your valour.

52. "Having taken Kalyan, and Bhimapuri too, you have, they say, struck down the great mosques of the Muslims.

53. "Even now the Muslims, those serpents, are angry with you, whose all you have taken and whom you have humiliated.

54. "Without thinking of your own strength, you have seized the Muslim teachers and fearlessly blocked the path of the learned.

55–57. "Since you yourself bear fearlessly the insignia of an emperor, mount a golden throne you have no right to, yourself show favour and punish men, and, being your own master, bow to no one among those who should be bowed to, being proud — and since your course is hard to check, and you fear no one — for all this I have been sent by the valiant Adil.

58. "The army of six kinds that has come with me by Adil's order now urges me to war.

59. "Muse Khan and the others, eager to fight you, urge me on here, wanting to take Jayavalli.

60. "So, lord of the earth, make peace on my terms, and give up the whole of your land, movable and immovable.

61–62. "The Simha hill; the great and strong Loha hill with its heights; the hill of Purandar; likewise the city of Chakravati; and the country around the Nira and the Bhima — bow to the lord of Delhi, of boundless might, and quickly give them up.

63. "Jayavalli, which you seized by force from Chandraraj — Ali Shah asks you for that too."

64. Having heard in private the contents of the letter brought by the envoy, that unrivalled hero, the jewel of all the race of kings, is said to have settled on a certain plan in his own heart.

65. Listen, wise men, as I tell you all of it, for your good: what answer to that letter the king sent, after thinking of the counsel best for the whole world, the counsel he wished for; and how the proud Afzal, all ready, came from Wai to that forest.

*Thus ends the eighteenth canto, called "The Sending of the Message", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
