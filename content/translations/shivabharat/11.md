# Canto 11

*The learned men said:*

1. After sending his son Shiva, with his counsellors, to the Pune country, what did King Shah do while living in the Karnataka country?

2. And how did Mahmud himself treat Shah, who had conquered his enemies and whose zeal in war was famous?

*The prince of poets said:*

3. By the use of the six measures of policy and by the strength of his various counsels, Shah brought the whole Karnataka country under his control.

4. Jagadeva, seeking to bow in friendship, received his command on his head as if it were a flower.

5. Even the invincible lord of Madhura became obedient to him; the lord of Mahishur too submitted to his authority.

6. Virabhadra, whose fine throne had been seized by force by the wicked Randulla Khan, sat on it again under his protection.

7. Using one counsel or another as occasion required, that wise man by his power removed the Muslims' fear of many people.

8. Through King Shah's counsel, Randulla, very hard for others to bear, carried out all his master's business.

9–10. Then, when the general Randulla had gone the way of Time, whatever general Adil sent there to bring the kings of Karnataka quickly under his control followed Shah's lead, wishing for success.

11. Then the son of Ibrahim, out of pride and choosing the wrong course, ordered Mustafa Khan to seize King Bhosle.

12–15. Then, outroaring the ocean with the thunder of his drums, filling the quarters with the cries of victory of his warriors, scaring the clouds with his fluttering banners, frightening the clouds with the raised trunks of his elephants, blotting out the sun with the dust raised by the hooves of his horses, drying up rivers with his flowing streams of troops, levelling the earth as if its heights and hollows were made one, he reached the Karnataka country, held by enemy warriors.

16–18. Hearing that Mustafa Khan was coming — sun of the land of Khurasan, a man of many deceits, who held the rank of general and enjoyed Adil's trust, famous, very proud, of great family — King Shah, though he did not trust him, made a show of trust and went out eagerly with his army to meet him.

19. As they showed each other more and more affection, a great festival took place on the road at their meeting, like a meeting of friends.

20. There the two gave each other clothes, ornaments, elephants and horses in abundance.

21. Then the strong King Bhosle encamped his army near the camp of Mustafa Khan's army.

22. Whenever Mustafa Khan, seeking a weak point, looked at King Shah, he found him fully on guard.

23. To win his trust, Mustafa made a show of friendship and put Shah first in every piece of business.

24–28. Rising quickly to greet him, going far out to meet him, gladly embracing him and clasping his hands, giving him half his seat, turning his face to him, speaking with a smile, looking at him with eyes eager for affection, revealing the plans of his various schemes, giving him great precedence in every matter, honouring him with gifts and presents, praising and flattering him, joking with him with much humour, telling him of spiritual matters, rousing his sense of what was good for him, and telling him of his own affairs — in these ways the Muslim showed him trust day after day.

29. Then that guardian of the army, skilled in policy, called together all the captains of his army and spoke these words in private:

*Mustafa Khan said:*

30. "When a servant lives on a man and eats his food, his life belongs to that man and is never his own.

31. "Without learning there is no greatness; without genius no poetry; and without a master's favour no wish is ever seen fulfilled.

32. "So those who know policy call that man blessed who, for his master's sake, gives up his own life.

33. "To those devoted to serving their master, no kinsman, friend, relative or brother born of the same womb — not even a father — should come first.

34. "What man, if he is pleased when his master is pleased and ruined when his master is angry, would not serve him with an undivided mind?

35. "So let all of us now, standing firm in this great rule, strive together for Mahmud's good.

36–37. "My proud master Mahmud has sent me the message: 'Shah must be seized.' So, as long as the strong King Bhosle remains unaware, we who want our own good must do it.

38. "When this night has passed, at daybreak, all of you together with your own soldiers, seize the king."

39. Told this by Mustafa for the success of his aim, the captains of the army went each to his own camp.

40. On the very night they made this plan, great portents appeared in Shah's camp.

41. Horses wept, elephants cried out piteously, and banners broke without warning with a harsh crack.

42. Drums sounded harshly though no one beat them, and whirlwinds spread dust suddenly.

43. Hail fell all around without clouds, and lightning flashed in a cloudless sky.

44. At lamp-lighting time the lamps would not light, and men's faces and minds were not glad.

45. Near the army jackals made inauspicious cries, and dogs, raising their faces, howled most dismally.

46. Owls came very close again and again and hooted, and packs of wolves suddenly made cruel cries.

47. In every temple the images of the gods trembled, and at midnight the cows suddenly lowed.

48. Many such signs foretelling fear arose, yet the king, in the power of fate, did not become wary.

49. At midnight spies came and reported: "The commanders are standing ready in their own camps, and Mustafa has held long counsel with the chiefs of the army in the middle of the night." Yet, though he heard it, the very strong King Shah, being subject to fate, did not do what the moment required.

*Thus ends the eleventh canto in the Samhita, the Anupurana brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
