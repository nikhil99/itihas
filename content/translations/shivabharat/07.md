# Canto 7

*The prince of poets said:*

1. His parents did not know that the lovely child at play in a child's body was Urukrama, Vishnu, more than human.

2–3. Come down to the earth's surface to end the earth's burden, playing in a child's form in the house of King Shah, delighting his mother and the people with his beauty, the Self of all performed there his various deeds.

4–5. He who, to do what Indra wished, had again and again slain the sons of Diti in battle after battle with his four arms raising their weapons, and who sleeps, as if tired, in the middle of the ocean of milk — he now cried for his mother's breast.

6. Playing as a child and crawling on emerald floors, he would try hard to catch his own reflection.

7. As he crawled on his knees, the crystals set in the courtyard took on the colour of rubies from the glow of his lotus-like soles.

8. As he crawled about the courtyard of the house, grey with dust, he delighted his mother with the jingle of his jewelled anklets.

9. Playing and crawling eagerly about the jewelled courtyard, he vied at every step, alas, with his own reflection.

10. He by whose grace all the gods drink nectar — ah, he too ate a soft lump of earth and was delighted.

11. The god who, tricking Bali, crossed the three worlds in three strides, now had to struggle, alas, to get over the threshold of the house.

12. The lord who is himself the support of the seven worlds stood up holding on to his nurse's finger.

13–14. Seeing the sun's disc reflected on the walls of white crystal, he pointed it out with his forefinger, crying "Take it in your hand and give it to me", and, as if beguiled by his own innocence, made his mother laugh.

15–16. Seeing him at the door of the house like a young elephant, his head grey with the dust he had thrown up with the lotus of his hand, adorned with the first shoots of teeth like jasmine buds, the nurse who had come to teach him stood amazed.

17. Delighted by the nurse clapping her hands, he danced in many ways with a smiling face.

18. He who had taught the creator the Vedas with their marks of accent now learned the names of things from his nurse's mouth.

19. Pleased at his own wish, he would quickly take off the ornaments on his body, one after another, and give them to his nurses.

20. Though master of them all, he set aside his father's elephants and horses, and liked best his elephants and horses of clay.

21. Shining with a tuft of hair, he ran after the peacocks to get their tail feathers.

22. Imitating the cries of peacocks, parrots and cuckoos, he made people think those very birds were calling.

23. Standing beside his loving nurse, this tiger-cub of a child would suddenly roar and give her a fright.

24. Untiring, he sometimes whirled round and round like a spinning top; sometimes, delighted, he neighed like a horse.

25. Sometimes he trumpeted loudly like an elephant.

26. Sometimes, proud of himself, he imitated a kettledrum with deep, sweet sounds that filled heaven and earth.

27. Sometimes he had the boys build tall piles of earth and said, "These are my forts."

28. Sometimes, playing hide-and-seek, he hid in a corner of the house, and when his friends searched him out and touched him he laughed.

29. Sometimes he struck the ball, which he had dropped from his hand and which bounced up again and again, to knock it down.

30–31. Sometimes, his black eyes fixed on the ball he had thrown high, as it fell from the sky far above, looking up with his face raised, beating the ground with both feet as if dancing, he caught it in his raised hands.

32. He at the sight of whom men are instantly freed from the whirl of birth never felt dizzy from his whirling.

33. Though his nurses forbade him and threatened him with a finger, the lion-cub of King Shah kept to all his childish games.

34. Told "Eat," he would not eat; told "Drink," he would not drink; coaxed by his nurses with "Sleep," he would not sleep.

35. Lost in this game and that, he, the supreme Self of the world, went far off even when his mother called him.

36. Wherever Shiva went, absorbed in the delight of his childish play, the gods led by Vasava followed and guarded him.

37. The glorious one, the treasure of compassion, the support of the gods, sung in the Vedanta and famed in the Puranas as the Primal Person, who was born, they say, in King Shah's palace to remove the earth's fear — he took on the great beauty of the age called childhood.

*Thus ends the seventh canto, called "The Description of His Childhood Games", in the Anupurana composed by Kavindra Paramananda.*
