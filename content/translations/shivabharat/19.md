# Canto 19

*The prince of poets said:*

1–2. Placing everywhere his eye in the form of spies, Shiva watched carefully everything outside and inside. Then he called an envoy skilled in his work and sent this reply to the enemy:

*King Shiva said:*

3. "You who destroyed in battle all the kings of the Karnataka land have shown me today a kindness better than I deserve.

4. "Your strength of arm is unequalled, your might matchless. You have adorned the earth, and in you there is no guile.

5. "If you wish to see the riches of these forests, then come to Jayavalli and look at them.

6. "Your coming here is right for now, I think. Through it I shall become free of fear, and my fortune will grow.

7. "I think the army of the copper-faced men, terrible as it is, worth no more than a straw, and Adil's too, fierce as its valour is, without you.

8. "Come carefully along the road. I shall give you, when you ask, the hills and this Jayavalli too.

9. "Seeing you here, whom it is hard even to look at, I shall with an untroubled mind lay before you this sword in my hand.

10. "Your army, seeing this forest, which none can bend, will enjoy pleasures like those of the underworld's shade."

11. In this way Shiva, sending a message like a sutra, of few syllables but much meaning, dismissed his own envoy.

12. Hearing that message spoken by Shiva's envoy, the evil-minded man thought he had achieved his master's purpose.

13. Though he was at Vairata, the Muslim thought Jayavalli, three yojanas away, was already as good as in his hand.

14. Then, sitting in his hall of audience and calling his soldiers, the great-minded man made this politic speech:

*Afzal said:*

15. "That Shiva, wishing to make peace, is calling me there. Wanting to do my master's business, I must go from here.

16. "For us, with our zealous and well-armed soldiers all around us, all of us ever wakeful, there is no fear there.

17. "We will marshal our armies and go there in every way, ah, into that dreadful forest where not even the sun's light reaches.

18. "Resting there in the forests, the haunts of tigers and elephants, you will see moment by moment the shelter of the forest.

19. "There, seeing the very great forest on the mountain slopes, let my elephants play at will like wild ones.

20. "Even if the sky should fall or hundreds of thunderbolts strike, the mind of this Afzal will not change.

21. "I shall dig up the earth, I shall throw down the mountains, and I shall at once burn the forests with the fire of my wrath.

22. "I shall keep my vow and topple the gods. If in his folly he will not accept my fitting words, so be it."

23. Of the soldiers there, their minds struck down by fate, none contradicted him as he spoke thus, seated on his shining seat in the hall.

24. Then all the ministers, following policy, humbly addressed him as he stood ready to march:

*The ministers said:*

25. "Master, you must certainly do what you have in mind to do. But who can forbid what fate has settled?

26. "If he trusts you with a clean heart, let him come out of the forest of Jayavalli at once and meet you.

27. "Let him present all he has, to please you, and bow his own neck before you, bowing at every step.

28. "That bowman, who from boyhood has never bowed his head, even in a dream, to any lord except the god of the matted locks;

29. "who from childhood, endowed with boundless strength, has done deeds in this world beyond those of men, as we have often heard;

30. "who thinks it shameful in this world even to look at the faces of the Muslims, who bears the yoke of his own dharma and, plainly, never listens to their words —

31. "if he fearlessly calls you to that very spot, then we think there is some daring hidden in his heart.

32. "That inaccessible hill country, choked at every step with clumps of bamboo and cane, is no comfort to horses.

33. "How will elephants go by those mountain paths, choked with hundreds of trees at the top, where climbing up and down is cramped?

34. "You are the very life of Ali Shah, a thorn in the heart of the lord of Delhi, the master of a fine army. Do not enter that terrible forest.

35. "He, like a yogi who has mastered food, play and posture, knows the hidden mind of everyone, yet keeps his own secret.

36. "He leaps from mountain tops onto those within the ramparts and kills his crooked enemies like the king of birds.

37. "His going is not known, and a yojana is to him as nothing. He can tell in his own mind a man who hates him.

38–39. "The goddess Tulaja, whom you, the tormentor of the world, once insulted, always gives him her help. How can you, proud as you are, go into that forest guarded by that enemy with his many armies?"

40. Hearing their words, he, his mind blind with hatred and his eyes red with passion, brushed them aside and spoke to them.

*Afzal said:*

41. "That arrogant enemy who has offended against us from his birth — how will he come of his own accord to meet us?

42. "When you praise that man as one who does superhuman deeds, I see that you have not grasped my own manhood.

43. "I who crushed the armies of the Karnataka kings under the hooves of my galloping horses, and who have now humbled the race of the gods by breaking their shrines in place after place — that man, all-capable though he is, will at the sight of me suddenly be troubled by the fire of my wrath, and out of fear will make a treaty with me.

44. "That wide forest, covered with many trees and full of enemy warriors fighting there, which he would have you think so dangerous — that fearsome forest I shall level at once." Saying this, that evil-minded man set out, rejecting the counsel of those who wished him well.

*Thus ends the nineteenth canto, called "The March of Afzal", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
