# Canto 16

*The prince of poets said:*

1–2. Then, when Mahmud was greatly content at getting what he wanted, but Shiva had himself given up the Simha hill for his father's sake, Shiva, skilled in counsel, called near him the Brahmin Sonaji, his elder in birth and a knower of counsel, and said:

*Shiva said:*

3. "Surely that great-minded great king, whom people know to be blessed and the foremost of men who know, does not really know me.

4. "If an ignorant man does not know a good man's virtues, he is merely unfortunate. But if a man who knows does not know them, that pains the good man's heart.

5. "If he knew me, he would not have given away the Simha hill. Otherwise, who could have taken by force this fort that was in my hand?

6. "What shall I say of that great-souled man, who, though no fool, gave the Simha hill to Adil, his enemy?

7. "Though he himself governs everyone, alas, trusting his enemies he slept at ease in his own house, and then was seized in his own house by his enemies.

8. "An enemy should never be despised even if he is weak. A burning lamp can be put out even by a moth.

9. "A moth dies even as it destroys the lamp; who but a man born blind would see the two as equal?

10. "Surely even an ant can enter an elephant's trunk and be the death of it. So an enemy should never be thought small.

11. "Ah! This strong-armed man, though he knows great policy, simply ignores his enemies. That is his great fault.

12–13. "I am fully ready to take vengeance for the long suffering in prison of my revered father Shah, who was captured by the enemy. From today my fame will spread through the destruction of the Muslims.

14. "The enemy is afraid even of the man who is greatly afraid of him; it goes without saying that the enemy fears the man who does not fear him at all.

15. "Surely I must kill those Muslim captains. These arrows of mine, sharpened on the whetstone, are eager for it.

16. "My mind is now eager to take the city called Vijaya. So I tell you today: have the horsemen made ready at once."

*Sonaji said:*

17. "Enmity with the strong ends badly; a store of sin ends badly; reproach of the revered and of elders ends badly.

18. "Your father made an enemy of the strong and acted stubbornly. Since he was not careful, his capture was what he deserved.

19. "That great king is mighty, and his ministers are men of good conduct; yet the enmity he made with the strong was against policy.

20. "When a weak man makes enmity with a strong one, the strong man is fully able to defeat him.

21. "Gifts are prescribed for an enemy who is stronger, conciliation for an equal, punishment for one who is weaker; division is held to apply to all.

22. "Through division an equal enemy, growing weaker day by day, gives up his equality, and a stronger enemy his superiority. Who would not praise division?

23. "O king, division is right even against a weaker enemy, for it makes the weak weaker still, which is all to the good in war.

24. "If a weak enemy is conciliated in the thought that he is suffering, he swells up and thinks himself great.

25. "O king, the strength of the weak and the weakness of the strong are seen to come about through the power of changing times.

26. "So a king who keeps in mind the view of Varuna, lord of the waters, punishes a weak enemy.

27. "Means are held to be of two kinds, effective and ineffective. The second is barren; the first bears fruit.

28. "Women take away a man's strength; wine brings delusion; gambling takes away wealth; avarice ruins one's work.

29. "Harsh speech brings ill fortune; hunting is sinful; and harsh punishment, lord of men, brings very great disgrace.

30. "So one should give up these seven vices, for even one of them, alas, leads to ruin of judgement.

31. "If even one of them ruins judgement, what man who indulges in many of them will not go to the depths?

32. "Fortune abandons the man who is averse to the shastras of policy, lazy in the weight of his own affairs, and whose mind is sunk in vice.

33. "When he grows careless, the treasurers loudly rob his treasury, and evil-minded officials oppress his realm.

34. "Delusion grows in the king addicted to vices, and as delusion grows, his sense of what is fitting greatly declines.

35. "As his sense of the fitting declines, all people despise him, and those appointed to tasks do not quickly carry them out.

36. "Then, angry, he utters harsh words loudly and wounds even those gathered in his assembly day after day.

37. "Then, their hearts wounded by insult moment after moment, they speak ill of him behind his back and mock him.

38–39. "Then some leave him, some grow indifferent to him, some go over to the enemy's side and live there at ease, some, thinking him short-tempered, curse him, and some who do not leave him no longer serve him as before.

40. "Through attachment to the seven vices, not knowing his enemy's deceit or strength, he thinks himself mighty out of mere conceit.

41. "Alas! He whose mind is attached to vices becomes like this, and when he marches out for victory he is at once overcome by enemies.

42. "Hills are not heavy; a father alone is held heavy. That is why, mighty as you are, you gave up the Simha hill.

43. "If that lord of the earth is released once it has been given to this enemy, then, though you gave the Simha hill, it is not truly given.

44. "What has that enemy, who thinks the Simha hill and the city of Bengrul equal to the Meru that is Shah, really gained?

45. "You have now begun on the circle of conquest, and to a king who strides out boldly the whole earth belongs.

46. "The king, then the minister, friends, ample wealth, the realm, forts and armies: these are the seven limbs of a kingdom.

47. "A kingdom with all these limbs complete is praised by the good; if it lacks even one, it is mocked by others.

48–49. "The king is the head; the minister the mouth; wealth and army the two arms; the realm the body; the friends the strong joints; the forts the strong bones. Thus the wise have named the seven limbs of the kingdom.

50–53. "Of this seven-limbed kingdom, dharma is called the soul; counsel the breath; policy the strength and impolicy the weakness; proper punishment the courage; adharma the disease; the enemy's insolence the intoxication; mutual division the long life; unity the beloved company of the people; foresight the eye; valour the bright form; a clear mind the life; enmity with the strong the obstacle; heedlessness the sleep; and vigilance the waking.

54. "Enmity is said to be of two kinds, equal and unequal: the first with an equal, the second with a stronger.

55. "In equal enmity neither side can truly win, being equal. In unequal enmity victory surely goes to the stronger, because he is stronger.

56. "Because of Fateh Khan's rout and Muse Khan's killing, Mahmud, lord, envies you day and night.

57. "See, O king: Adil and the lord of Delhi, Muslims next to your own country and strong, plot against you day and night.

58. "Surely for the man who has these two hostile neighbours on both sides, sitting still in this place is not fitting now.

59. "So, lord of the world, take your stand in a very inaccessible place and strive to conquer the world. What is unconquerable for you, Shiva?

60. "Kailasa was taken by the destroyer of Kandarpa, Meru by the bearer of the thunderbolt, and the waters of the ocean by the conqueror of the demons, for shelter.

61. "People do not count a fort impregnable merely because it is a fort. Its impregnability lies in its master being impregnable.

62. "A fort is made impregnable by its master, and a master by his fort. Without both, an enemy becomes impregnable.

63. "Have all the forts you possess at once made as impregnable as possible in every way."

64. Hearing these words of Sonaji, that best of princes, worthy of honour even from the very great, held them worthy of honour.

65. Then, holding sovereignty for the protection of the three worlds, hater of his father's enemies, the younger son of Shah, that jewel, fearless, intent on great slaughter of the Muslims, thought of the whole earth as resting in the palm of his hand.

*Thus ends the sixteenth canto in the Anupurana brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda of Nivasa.*
