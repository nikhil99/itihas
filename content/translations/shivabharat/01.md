# Canto 1

*Salutation to Shri Ganesha. Salutation to Shri Samba Sadashiva. Salutation to the goddesses Shri Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati. May it be well.*

1. Again and again I bow to Govinda, whose lotus feet shine with the garlands of mandara flowers worn on the bowed heads of the greatest gods.

2. Once upon a time Paramananda, an excellent Brahmin, went to the city of Varanasi on a pilgrimage.

3–4. There, where all beings are released yet release itself is never released, where the Lord himself teaches the saving Brahman, this Brahmin who knew his duty performed the pilgrim's rites, beheld the great god Maheshvara, and sat down on the holy bank of the Bhagirathi.

5–7. He sat there in the lotus posture, blazing with the radiance of Brahman: a knower of all the shastras, famous, learned in the inner science, foremost of the reciters of the Puranas, son of Bhatta Govinda, gifted with mastery of speech by the grace of the goddess Ekavira — Paramananda, prince of poets, bliss in bodily form. When they saw him the learned men who lived in Kashi were filled with joy.

8. He rose to meet those noble men, greeted them, and honoured them with due ceremony as guests arriving at his home.

9. Those best of the twice-born sat down around him, wishing to hear the famous story of King Shiva.

10. Then all those wise men, delighted, said to him, the maker of fine poems and an incarnation of Brihaspati:

*The learned men said:*

11. "The king who rules this earth, lord of the kings of the mountains, who won his kingdom by the grace of Tulaja, great in austerity;

12. "born of a portion of Vishnu, and especially of the guardians of the world; high-minded, clear of soul, valiant, master of his senses;

13. "terrible even to the terrible, the boundary-mark of all who bear the bow; wise, noble in conduct, glorious, of wondrous prowess;

14. "accomplished, grateful, virtuous, self-perfected, bearing the marks of fulfilment; a speaker of true speech and a hearer of great discernment;

15. "protector of gods, Brahmins and cows, destroyer of the wicked, guardian of those who take refuge, doer of good to his subjects —

16. "the history of this king, made in many cantos, which you have brought to light by the grace of the goddess,

17. "rich in noble diction, full of meaning, wonderful, endowed with sweetness and the other virtues of style and adorned with figures of speech,

18. "filled with the meaning of the shastras of dharma and of statecraft, heard of in every land, a new Purana as it were —

19. "— that whole history, free of every fault and possessed of every mark of excellence, tell to us, O knower of all, whose vows are praised."

20. Thus entreated by the circle of wise men living in Varanasi, the righteous prince of poets, best of speakers, replied.

*The prince of poets said:*

21–22. Bowing to the blessed Ekavira, to Ganesha and Sarasvati, and to my true guru, the great adept who grants success even to the adepts, I shall tell the history of the wise great king Shiva, as the Bharata tells the history of Bharata's line.

23. Wise men should hear the glories of King Shiva, which take away the sins of the Kali age and captivate the hearts of men.

24–27. This hero, lord of the mountains, triumphs: Shiva, the great king of the South, son of King Shahaji, Narayana himself made manifest, the splitter of the Muslims' pride. Once, graciously, he said to me, who am devoted to him: "Whatever deeds I have done on earth, and whatever I am doing, describe them all, wise one. Beginning with my grandfather, the famous King Maloji, set out this great story, O fortunate one."

28–30. Welcoming those words of his, O best of the twice-born, I promised, came home, and thought to myself: "Ah, how shall I make a great work like the Bharata, on this wonder of a life of Shiva?" As I pondered thus with a steady mind for a long while, the blessed goddess herself came to me and said:

*The goddess said:*

31. "Prince of poets, do not worry. I am on your side. It was by my command that this king laid this task on you.

32. Comforting me in this way, the compassionate goddess of our family, the blessed four-armed one, entered my heart.

33. From that moment, as I watched, the whole of the Word, Brahman as speech, came alive together with its meaning and settled on the tip of my tongue.

34. Seeing plainly before me all things past and to come, I became like a being more than human.

35. Then, thinking that I had done what I had to do, I made this sinless account of great merit.

36–41. In it are told the greatness of Shambhu, the great god, and of Tulaja, slayer of the wicked demons; dharma, wealth, desire and liberation, each in its place; and the glory of the holy places, rightly set out. In it are the many wars of Shiva, who came down to earth to destroy the Muslims, against those Muslims; the surpassing greatness of gods, Brahmins and cows; the pure and wonderful deeds of kings; the marks of elephants, horses and forts; and the everlasting science of kingship, set out in full. Listen with undivided minds, best of hearers, as I tell of that sinless line of the Sun from its beginning.

42–43. In the southern quarter, in the line of the Sun, was born the glorious King Maloji, a lord of men bright as the sun itself. Clear of soul and bearer of the yoke of his own dharma, that lord of the Maharashtra land ruled the country of Maharashtra.

44. Lotus-eyed, with the valour of the lord of Lakshmi, deep in virtue, he shone as he delighted his subjects.

45. That righteous man made his home in the holy country of Pune and, by the Bhima river, made his kingdom more prosperous than his own.

46–47. The banks of the Bhima were trampled by the hooves of his leaping horses; the roar of his kettledrums shook the ocean; his splendour scorched the circle of enemy kings. That mighty man by his own strength enjoyed the whole region of the Sahyadri.

48. Unbearable to his enemies, foremost of all bowmen, born of a portion of Narayana, he was like a second Dhananjaya.

49. He married with due rites the illustrious Uma, born of a great family, as Satyavan married Savitri.

50. He prized her greatly, for she was endowed with beauty and virtue to match his own, as Aja, son of Raghu, prized the virtuous Indumati.

51. As if it were a gift that Parvati had given of her own self, the virtuous lady adorned the name Uma.

52. Then, rich as Kubera and blessed with that beautiful wife, the wise and accomplished king performed many kinds of righteous works.

53. In his kingdom there were always sacred fires, houses of free food, sacrifices with ample fees to the priests, and great gifts.

54. To please Shambhu, that devotee of Shambhu dug a lake of sweet water on the hill of Shambhu, as wide as an ocean.

55–56. That righteous man built in many places mansions high as golden Meru with lofty gateways, delightful gardens rich in trees, long step-wells with flights of golden stairs, water-sheds and rest-houses.

57. As the Ganga followed Bhagiratha, so a great and mighty army of four arms followed that strong man.

58. Princes and kings bowed before that exalted king as reeds bow before the wind beside the ocean.

59–60. At this time the righteous Nizam Shah ruled the earth, living at the fort of Devagiri. All the kings of the South, the Yadava king and the rest, always served that lord of the Muslims.

61. At the same time Adil Shah too, a Muslim, ruled a kingdom, living with Muslims around him in the city called Vijaya.

62. Then, after some time, for some reason, a great and violent quarrel arose between Adil Shah and the Nizam.

63. Then the shrewd Nizam, hearing that King Maloji was as strong as Death, decided to seek his help.

64. Then, to do what the Nizam wished, the unrivalled King Maloji went to Devagiri and lived there.

65. His brother too, Vithoji Raje, terrible in valour like Bhima, joined him there with his army and waited on the lord of Dharagiri.

66. Glad at heart that the two had come to him, the Nizam honoured them again and again with kind words and gifts.

67. Whoever rose up as an enemy of the Nizam Shah, the strong-armed King Maloji rooted out.

68. Vithoji Raje too, with the might of Shakra, always helped the Nizam and did what he wanted done.

69. The Nizam had many helpers, yet Maloji stood above them all.

70. Placing the kingdom inherited from his forefathers in the hands of his ministers, he governed another territory granted him by the Nizam Shah.

71. Then, as he lived with his wife and longed for a son to make his royal fortune shine, many days went by.

72. Wanting offspring, that king and his lawful wife took a great vow and worshipped Mahadeva, god of gods.

73. Then, after a long time, the beloved queen of that mighty man conceived, to his joy.

74. In the tenth month, at an auspicious hour, she happily gave birth to a wonderful son glowing with the marks of a king.

75–76. Fine-nosed, wide-eyed, broad-browed, with smooth curling hair, broad-chested, long-armed, high-shouldered, golden in colour, with reddish lotus hands and feet, he lit up the house with his own radiance from the moment he was born.

77. When they saw him the nurses were overjoyed and in their excitement sent word to the king through the servants of the inner palace.

78. Hearing that great news of a son's birth, he felt as if every limb had been bathed in a shower of nectar.

79. Then, eager and swimming in joy, the king came and gazed on the face of his tender young son.

80–83. Then, delighted, he had the birth rites performed for him with blessings by his own family priest. When this prince, bright as Kumara, was born, auspicious instruments sounded, courtesans danced, singers with smooth and steady voices sang songs, bards loudly recited his titles, and the best of the twice-born gave their joyful blessings, which never fail. In house after house a great festival was held.

84–85. To the people who came asking, the lord then gave great pearls, coral, jewelled ornaments, gold, cloth of gold, cattle, horses and elephants; and to them he seemed a wish-granting tree in human form.

86. Then, on a fine day free of evil influences and named by the astrologers, the father himself named the boy Shah.

87. Day by day the lotus-faced child grew, increasing the joy of his parents' eyes with his play.

88. Then, when two years had passed, the king's wife bore a second son, like joy made flesh.

89. For him too the learned performed the rites, and the lord gave him the name Sharif, as the holy men had declared.

90. Those two boys, Shah and Sharif, named by the holy men, lamps of their family, grew up, and the family's fortune grew with them.

91. Glad at heart, he and his kin rejoiced in those two sons who bore all the marks of kingship, and the lord felt that his royal line had put out new shoots on the earth.

92. When a wise man hears this story of the birth of the splendid royal princes, famous in the world, which I have told, and which takes away the stain of the Kali age, he at once gains what he desires.

*Thus ends the first canto, called "The Birth of the Princes", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda.*
