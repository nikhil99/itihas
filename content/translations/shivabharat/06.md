# Canto 6

*The prince of poets said:*

1. Then Hari, lord of yogis, wishing himself to enter her womb, graciously showed himself to Shah's wife.

2–7. Once, in a dream, the lovely queen saw the god sitting on her lap in the form of a child at play: he whom the hosts of gods worship; dark, four-armed, holding conch, discus, mace and lotus; the Shrivatsa mark on his chest and the Kaustubha jewel at his throat; wearing the Vaijayanti garland and yellow silk; a shining jewelled crown on his head and glittering makara earrings; his cheeks bright with a gentle smile, his face serene; long-eyed as a white lotus, fine-nosed, with auspicious marks; every limb a playground of beauty, wonderful; Kamala at his side; adorned with a garland of wild flowers; the god in whom all gods dwell, decked with every ornament; his lotus feet marked with the thunderbolt, lines, banner and parasol.

8. Not long after, the wife of King Shah bore in her womb this mighty form, a portion of the great Vishnu.

9. With that great radiance inside her womb she shone like a bank of autumn cloud with the sun hidden within.

10. Bearing that radiant child in her womb, the daughter of the lord of the Yadavas, alas, adorned the whole earth.

11. Then, longing for the cravings of pregnancy and slow with the weight of the child, she with her broad hips found even her ornaments heavy.

12. Her pale face, beyond compare, by its brightness mocked the orb of the autumn moon.

13. It is no wonder that the beautiful woman's body grew heavy, since the god, guru of the world, had himself entered her womb.

14. Her friends who waited on her thought that pale-faced, pregnant lady quite unlike the goddesses.

15–18. Then day after day she had many cravings: to ride horses, elephants and elephant-mounts; to sit firm on a golden throne under a white parasol; to have banners raised and fine chowries waved; to hear the sound of kettledrums; to do heroic deeds; to bear arrows, bows, spears, swords and armour; to win victory; to make great gifts; and to set dharma on its feet.

19–25. Then, in the birth chamber, the radiant and divine queen shone in her divine form. Skilled old women of good family and character attended her day and night, in the service of the prince; famous attendants followed her wishes and always did what was good for her; wise women with nectar in their hands, trusted ones, nursed the pregnant lady. The room was made with plastered walls bright as nectar and drawn with svastikas; a net of pearls hung at the edge of a shining canopy; fresh leaves were fastened there and white mustard seed scattered; golden pots filled with water and full of peace; deities drawn on both sides of the door; many auspicious lamps placed all around; and all the medicines needful gathered in it.

26–31. In the Shalivahana Shaka year counted by "earth, arrows, breaths and moon" — that is, 1551 — in the year named Shukla, when the sun had begun its northern course, in the cool season, in the auspicious month of Phalguna, in the dark half, on the third day, at night, in a lagna of fine omen, with five planets standing favourable and exalted, foretelling sovereignty over the whole world, firm and splendid — she bore a son: of boundless beauty, golden in colour, free of sickness, with a lovely neck and high shoulders, his face framed by curls that matched the bees for blackness, his eyes as fine as lotuses, his nose like a fresh palasha bud, his face naturally smiling, his voice deep as thunder, broad-chested and strong-armed.

*(Shaka 1551, Phalguna vadya 3, corresponds to 19 February 1630.)*

32. Then gods and men rejoiced by the thousand, and his drums beat of themselves with their sound.

33. Instruments of many kinds were played in house after house; the quarters grew clear, and so did the rivers.

34. Fragrant, cool winds blew gently, and the fire, appeased, took up the offerings poured into it.

35–36. Shruti and Smriti, Steadiness, Wisdom, Beauty, Peace, Patience and Compassion, Policy, Love, Achievement, Fame, Success, Shri and Sarasvati, Contentment, Nourishment, Power, Modesty, Knowledge and Progeny — these goddesses came together and stood around that god.

37–38. When the great King Shah had gone to another province to fight the proud Daryakhan, Pathan, the lord of the world was born in the Bhosle line, to favour the gods and to restrain the demons.

39. Then the family priest, who knew the rules, at once performed in due form the birth rites for the unborn one who had now been born.

40. And the reciters of hymns pronounced the blessing with hymns over him, who was himself able to sustain the whole world.

41. Then, when Vishnu had come down there in a human body, even the night became like day with the great mass of his radiance.

42–47. The Adityas, Vasus and Vishvedevas, the Rudras with the Maruts, the Yakshas, Sadhyas, Gandharvas and Vidyadharas, the cows led by Nandini, the elephants Airavata and the rest, the divine sages Narada and the others, and the Apsaras; Indra, Agni, Yama, Nairrita, the lord of the waters, the wind, the lord of wealth and Mahesha, the guardians of the quarters; the two Ashvin gods, Sun and Moon; the other planets with the constellations, in auspicious forms; the ghatika, muhurta, day and night, fortnight, month, season and year; the divine and human ages and the Manvantaras — all came near, and themselves pronounced blessings over the lord of the world, who had come down to lighten the earth's burden.

48–51. On the fifth, sixth, eighth and ninth days the family priest, purified, worshipped inside the birth chamber with their proper mantras Ganesha; the goddess Shashthi, giver of birth; Jivantika; Skanda; Narayana; the flute; Baladeva and the plough; the bow, arrows, sword and other weapons, and bowed, saying "Protect the child." Outside the house he gave many offerings to the guardian of the field, to ghosts, rakshasas, yoginis and the guardians of the quarters.

52–58. After the sixth-night vigil of the child who wakes the three worlds was over, on the tenth day, when people knew of the great one's birth, the holy rivers — Tamraparni, Kaveri, Tungabhadra, Malapaha, Krishna, Kakudmati, Vena, Nira, Bhimarathi, Godavari, Gayatri, Pravara, Vanjula, Purna, Payoshni, Tapi, the great river Narmada, Kshipra, Charmanvati, Bhadra, Yamuna, Vetravati, Bhagirathi, Chandrabhaga, Gomati, Gandaki, Iravati, Vipasha, Shatadru, Sarasvati, Vitasta, Sarayu, Tamasa and Vadhusara — with the rivers Sindhu, Ghargara and Shona that grant release, and with Pushkara and the other lakes and the oceans, came joyfully in unseen form for his ritual bath.

59–60. Devasena, Shachi, Svaha, Samriddhi, Sarvamangala, Aditi, Vinata, Samjna, Savitri and Arundhati — these women of the family gathered there and bathed the new mother together with that child of matchless form.

61–62. Fortunate women of the household waved lamps before the mother, who wore a white garment, had kept vigil through the night, was adorned with ornaments and held the child in her lap, shining like the beauty of day with the newly risen sun.

63. Because that best of men was born on the summit of Shivagiri, the name Shiva became famous for him in the world.

64. "This strong child will do superhuman deeds here. He will kill the Muslims and spread a great fame.

65. "By the strength of his arm this victorious one will conquer the southerners and the westerners, the easterners and the northerners, and will establish self-rule.

66. "This bold one will bring even the ocean under his sway. Strong himself, with his forces he will bring tribute from strong men in every quarter.

67. "This mighty one will hold mountain forts, forest forts, water forts and land forts.

68. "Alas! By his blazing valour he will set his foot on the head of the lord of Delhi and make the world obey his command.

69–75. "He, whose course nothing can check in forts, roads, thickets, mountains, rivers or the sea, will conquer by his own bright valour the Pandyas, Dravidas, Latas, Karnatakas, Keralas, Karahatas, Vairatas, Andhras, Malavas, Abhiras and Gurjaras; the Aryavartas, many times overrun by the wicked Muslims; the Kurujangalas, Sauviras, Dhanvas, Saurashtras and Kosalas; the Bahlikas, Madras, Gandharas, Trigartas, Anartas and Saindhavas; the Kalingas, Kamarupas, Angas, Vangas, Kambojas and Kekayas; the Persians, Shibis, Shalvas, Pulindas, Arattas and Barbaras; the Kashmiris, Matsyas, Magadhas, Videhas, Utkalas and Tankanas; the Kiratas, Kashis, Panchalas, Chedis and Kuntalas; the Khashas, Shurasenas, Hunas, the people of the Himalaya, the Undras, Pundras and Lalitthas — and he will be a great and long-lived king.

76–78. "As Prithu was among the kings of old, as King Pururavas, as Ambarisha and Shibi, son of Ushinara; as Mandhata was born, as the lord of the Nishadhas; as Bharata, as King Bhagiratha; as Harishchandra, as Rama son of Dasharatha; as the lord of Mithila, as Yayati and Nahusha were —

79. "— and as Yudhishthira, the king of dharma, whose wealth was dharma: so will this Shiva, son of King Shah, be."

80. Thus in the assembly the astrologers, masters of every siddhanta, declared the birth-hour of this prince.

81–90. His body and ornaments grew as his limbs grew; his lasting beauty and good fortune increased. A cap of gold, fringed with many-coloured gems and pearls, shone on his head; a golden pendant swung on his brow as he played; on his forehead hung a net of pearls and gems on bright threads; he wore armlets glittering with the rays of priceless diamonds and rubies; a tiger-claw set in gold hung at his throat with a string of black glass beads; a golden image of the goddess Jivantika, studded with gems, lay on his chest; bracelets of coral, sapphire and gold made a sweet sound on his two hands; his waist was tied with a sacred thread bringing joy; jewelled anklets and bells glittered on his feet; clever women had drawn lines of collyrium on his smooth eyes and a mark between his brows; he was dressed in a lovely little jacket of gold. Such was the smiling child whom the nurses lovingly looked after.

91. Though he moves within and without everything, the child was carried out, and his mother showed him the sun.

92. In due order the prince's rites of first sitting and first eating of rice were performed.

93. Then the victorious King Shah, having pierced Daryakhan with his arrows and humbled him, came to Shivagiri.

94. There, always risen in valour, he gazed with joy at the lotus-eyed brother of Shambhu.

95. Overjoyed, the king gave away cows, gold, elephants, horses and jewels.

96. At once, so much was given away there that for a long time no one who asked had any need to turn his face to another for anything.

97. Then the son of that sinless lord of the earth, a friend of the world and a remover of all darkness like the young sun lighting on lotuses with the rays of his hands, grew day by day and quickly gained great beauty.

*Thus ends the sixth canto, called "The Birth of King Shiva", in the Samhita, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda of Nidhivasa.*
