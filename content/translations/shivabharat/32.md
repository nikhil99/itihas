# Canto 32

*The prince of poets said:*

1. Then the son of Shah, setting going once more the ancient path of policy, by his own might brought the whole of the Prabhavali country into his own hand.

2. Shiva at once appointed Tryambak Bhaskar, skilled in deeds of fierce battle, famous and wise, to the office of governor of the Prabhavali country.

3. To him who had angrily taken away the wealth of Rajapur and conquered Shringarpur by his might, hundreds upon hundreds of the best of the enemy's soldiers, seeking refuge, bowed.

4. Then, seeing for a moment a famous hill lying in his own country, fit for the protection of that town, he gave it the name Pratit (Pratitgad).

5. Then the summer season, bringing joy with camphor, lotus stalks, moonlight, jasmine, sandalwood, champak and the rest, served that man of great joy.

6. Wearing the bow of the kesara flower, with a quiver of patali blossoms and arrows of flowers, the god of love, though he conquers the three worlds, did not steal Shiva's heart at all.

7. In the hot season, when the sun burned fiercely, the people, in pain, took shelter at the rivers, whose water, though quickly made lukewarm by the sun, kept cool beneath.

8. The fierce summer, burning hotter and hotter, drying up day by day the water of the ponds, left the cranes sitting in the mud and the rows of fish and tortoises dying.

9. With the breeze filled with the fragrance of the blossoming shirisha and patali, even the minds of the wise, though free of the objects of sense, were troubled by the fever of love.

*Here the text breaks off. The manuscript ends at this point, and nothing more of the poem is known.*
