# Canto 17

*The prince of poets said:*

1. Then Ali Shah called Afzal, the mainstay of his army, and himself spoke words suited to the occasion.

*Ali Shah said:*

2. "In this host of soldiers you are the one who does us good: a hater of Brahmins and gods, like a second Kali, the age of Time.

3. "Setting out before at the head of an army, you defeated in battle the kings born of the line of Ramaraja.

4. "While your valour, which scorches enemies, is awake, alas, even Shriranga Raja has lost his taste for the stage of battle.

5. "You, a hero, by your valour brought the lord of Karnapura under control in an instant, as a snake-charmer subdues a hissing serpent.

6. "Strong as Mount Mandara, you churned the city of Madhura, took the city of Kanchi, and carried off its gold.

7. "Even Virabhadra, made your servant at every step by your splendour, has forgotten the riches of parasol and chowrie.

8. "The lord of Simhala fears you, and so does the madman lord of Lanka; even the ocean serves me: this is the fruit of your manhood.

9. "Afzal, when you march, the seven mountains shake, the seven oceans are stirred, and the seven continents sink.

10. "Hearing of your manhood, even the lord of Delhi, though he has marched out, overcome with rage, sleeps neither day nor night.

11. "While you, so great a hero, unconquerable, are awake, the son of Shah, Shiva, plots against me day and night.

12. "Alas, through that zealous, proud hero, who is devoted to his own dharma, the dharma of the Muslims is being destroyed.

13. "Overrunning the land step by step like a lion overrunning a terrible forest, this man, who is his own master, takes no account of my command.

14. "Because this wicked man, whose mind is set on deceit, has the Sahya hills to shelter in, my heart has long borne something unbearable.

15. "If my father Mahmud had not held him back, he would have sunk the lord of Rajapuri in the ocean.

16. "He conquered Chandraraj with his sons and ministers, and took the town of Jayavalli as one seizes an unchecked planet.

17–18. "To make peace I gave Aurangzeb the Nizam Shah's lands, with their hills, forests and mines. That valiant man, his own master, ignoring us and Aurangzeb too, took them for himself by force.

19. "Coming without warning, intent on a robber's way of life, this man attacks my cities and villages and plunders them.

20. "A march that would take a day and night, a fortnight or a month, this fearless man covers in a moment.

21. "Deceitful by nature from his very boyhood, wakeful and of fierce valour, he scorns the Muslims.

22. "This man of great valour attacked the cities of the copper-faced men and punished Ahmadnagar and the other cities fiercely.

23. "Even the lord of Delhi, though he has with great effort secured the lasting possession of the Nizam's kingdom, does not think it safe for fear of him.

24. "His father, King Shah, whom my grandfather raised, cannot discipline that undisciplined son.

25. "When my valiant father had captured King Shah, this strong-armed man freed him by his strength alone.

26. "Though young, he does not shrink from fear of enemies, and he outdoes us with astonishing valour.

27. "Growing day by day in valour and fortune, he is sought out by kings who desire riches.

28. "Little by little the strong one is stepping forward. Will he not seize our kingdom and make it his own?

29. "The heroes I sent again and again to conquer him did not come back once they met that valiant man.

30. "He who makes an end of enemy heroes and fears nothing — I see no one but you who can conquer him.

31. "So you go, and seize him alive, as one seizes a hostile planet: Shiva, hard to catch, who has made the forts his own."

*The prince of poets said:*

32. When the proud Ali Shah had spoken to him in this way with great trust, Afzal, pleased, answered him with words to the point.

*Afzal said:*

33. "When a master gives an order with trust and with love, it is he who is truly its doer; the servant is only the instrument.

34. "You have today roused, by telling me the task, the power that lies awake in me to destroy the enemy's ranks.

35. "In sending this servant out on a fierce war, I think you have yourself shown him favour and made him your own.

36. "If a master does not tell his servants what to do, who will ever know whether they have valour or not?

37. "I shall bind that Shiva fast, who vies with you day and night, wicked as Death, and bring him to you."

38. "Entering the Karnataka country I conquered kings by the hundred. That victory of mine is worthless while I live without conquering him."

*The prince of poets said:*

39–40. Then Adil, eager to send there Afzal, who spoke in this way, proud of his great strength and ready to do at once the deed he had promised, honoured him with many rewards:

41–45. fine horses with jewelled saddles and leaping gait, elephants of the noble breed with their ornaments, armour, helmets, weapons of many kinds, bright clothes, his own titles, many litters that put the gods' cars to shame, couches of silver and of gold, ewers and spittoons, jewelled turban ornaments, pearl necklaces, armlets and the like, bracelets and girdles set with many gems, many fine elephants of noble breed from beyond the seas, and many treasure-chests: these Afzal received from Ali Shah.

46. Then the lord gave him his own sword, sharp as the edge of a thunderbolt, set in a jewelled scabbard, which had been in his own hand.

47. He wore the jewelled, ornamented dagger which his master had himself lovingly placed in his sash.

48. Then, after again and again saluting him with love in a manner fitting for departure, Afzal, like a moving mountain, set out.

49. As he went, bowing again and again, the master showed him favour at every step with a gaze full of kindness.

50. Having made that hero, honoured by heroes, general of the army, the great-minded Adil ordered other foolish men to help him.

51–54. Ambar, equal to Shambara; the valiant Yakut; the very proud Muse Khan; Hasan the Pathan; Randulla's son, also called Randulla; Ankush Khan, whose gait was like an unchecked elephant's; Khelkarna's Abyssinian, who had been bought as a son; the strong-armed Hilal, an elephant against enemy trees — these and other Muslims, with their armies and kinsmen, at their master's command quickly followed that general.

55–58. Following him came the Ghorpades, of terrible deeds, who had boldly conquered many enemy warriors and were fierce in battle; Pandhare Nayak and Kharate Nayak; Kalyan Yadav, leader of many soldiers; Mambaji Bhosle, full of zeal for war; the Ghatges of world-famous deeds, and the Kantes. These and other kings and vassals by the thousand, with armies of four arms, followed that general.

59. Then, as he went at the hour named by the astrologer, many inauspicious portents appeared, foretelling disaster.

60. Birds that went far on the left, crying loudly, seemed to tell him that his daring was in vain.

61–64. Alas, though it was midday, the sun shone dimly; the sky seemed ablaze and the quarters grew grey with dust; a great meteor suddenly fell from the sky; there was a crash of thunder from a sky without clouds; terrifying jackals howled from the eastern quarter; his banner broke; the vehicles were distressed; a wind blew contrary, raining stones and dust; and the elephant at the head of the army, though checked by the goad, ran wild.

65. Though these and other portents warned him, he, like Namuchi, did not give up his eagerness for battle.

66. Then, having set out from the city called Vijaya, he made his camp at a place half a yojana away.

67. Having come there with armies gathered on every side, he shone in his camp like an ocean of an army.

68–75. The general looked with great pride at his whole army: the circle of the sky covered by tall tents of costly colours; adorned with new canvas pavilions on raised poles; with its bright enclosures of tent-cloth walls; strewn with the spread of many-coloured seats; glittering with heaps of every kind of goods; its open spaces shaded by tall parasols; its fine horses tethered not far in front; its quarters filled with the trumpeting of herds of rutting elephants; its guards at every point, watchful day and night, with muskets, bows and other weapons, swords, shields, axes, spears and lances; terrible with the roar of many drums and instruments; full of the uproar of people going about their many tasks; and every man in it content in his proper place.

76. Proud in battle, greatly honoured by his master, splendid in valour, eager to conquer King Bhosle at once, Afzal, led by ill fate, seeing evil omens all around him at every step on the road, and hiding great guile in his heart, quickly reached the Vairata country.

*Thus ends the seventeenth canto, called "The Coming of Afzal", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda of Nidhivasa.*
