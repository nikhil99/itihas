# Canto 25

*The prince of poets said:*

1–2. Having defeated Rustam and the others and spread his bright fame, Shiva, wishing to bring Ali Shah's country quickly under his control, sent a great army with his general, and himself went back to the hill of Pranala to watch over it.

3. That general Netaji too, very famous, appointed by King Shiva, quickly brought Adil's realm under control.

4–9. Kavathe, Borgaon, Malgaon, Kundal, Ghogaon, Sattikir, Ad, Miraj, Gokak, Dodwad, Murwad, the great seat of Dharwad, Kshudrabandhapur, Shyamgaon (Sangaon), Mayil, Pargaon, Sangli, Kanad, Kurundwad, Kagal, Hebal, Hanuvalli, Hunwad, Raybag, Hukeri, Kandgaon, Haldi, Ghunki, Kini, Arag, Telsang, Kerur, Ambup, Kamalapur with Asangi, Tikote, and other great towns and old cities besides — the general, skilled in subduing, seized them and brought them under his control.

10–12. When the country had been laid waste in this way, the great army driven out, and the hill of Pranala and other forts had come under the enemy's power; and when even the copper-faced men, quickly summoned, had grown indifferent — Ali, sinking day by day in a sea of trouble, called the Abyssinian named Johar, lord of Karnapura, and quickly sent him to capture King Shiva.

13–14. Then he, with many thousands of horsemen of his own race, with countless elephants like mountains, and with the very strong Karnatak infantry, came to the hill of Pranala, guarded by King Shiva.

15–16. Rustam and Fazil too, though broken before, joined their own armies again at Adil's command and, with Sadat, of matching quality and character, joined Johar, who went quickly ahead.

17–18. Bajaraj Ghorpade; Kanta, the Karnatak Pidnayak; the son of Vali Khan, Bhai Khan, unconquerable in battle; Masud the Abyssinian; and others too, at Adil's order, came to the vicinity of Johar to take the hill of Pranala.

19–20. Then Johar, with Fazil and Rustam, those men steadfast in battle, each with his own horsemen, and with Bade Khan and other commanders of infantry, besieged that mountain, taking up a position on its eastern side.

21–23. Sadat, Masud, the strong-armed Bajaraj, Bhai Khan and the others, each placed at the head of his own troops; the mighty Pidnayak, with Karnatak fighters bearing shining shields and staves, and with other foot soldiers carrying guns, all around him, besieged the hill of Pranala and took up the western quarter.

24. Other armies too, at Johar's command, stood joined together, keeping to the southern and northern sides of that hill.

25. Shiva was on the mountain, very high, and Johar very low; yet the battle between them was a struggle of equals.

26. That Abyssinian fought the king for many months, but, beaten back every time, he gained no glory.

27–28. As the prince of poets, the sinless Brahmin, spoke thus, full of joy at the lotus feet of Shri Govinda, his heart overflowing with delight at the nectar of King Shiva's fame, the learned men who lived in Kashi gladly asked him again:

*The learned men said:*

29–31. When Ali, sending his envoy, quickly called the army of the lord of Delhi, which carried the pride of war, how large was it, and which leader guarded it? By what road did it come, where, and what work did it begin? And what counter did King Shiva make to it? Paramananda, wise one, tell us all of it.

*The prince of poets said:*

32–34. Seeing Shiva's rise and his own decline, when Adil loudly asked for help, wishing for it, the lord of Delhi, devoted to protecting those who seek refuge, ordered his own army — of great strength, very large, guarded by his strong maternal uncle named Shaista Khan, victor of hundreds of battles — to set out quickly; it was stationed at the foot of Dharagiri.

35. Then many leaders of the army, obedient to their master's command, set out well armed, with Shaista Khan at their head.

36–40. The proud Pathan named Shamas Khan, of famous valour; the son of Jafar Khan, named Namdar, hard to conquer; Gayasuddin Khan, Munim and Hasan; the valiant Mirza Sultan and Manuchehar; Turktaz and the cruel-souled Kubahat; Haidar Khan and Uzbek Khan, those two, eager for battle; Imam Virdi Khan and Lodi Khan, hard to withstand; the two Pathans, and two Maulads and two Dilavars likewise; Abdul Beg, Bhangad and Khoja Ambar; and Johar again, and the valiant Khoja Sultan;

41–55. Siddi Fateh and Fatehjang, skilled on the stage of battle; the wrathful Kartalab, Ghazi Khan and others; Bhavsingh, the mighty son of Shatrushalya, and his brothers the kings Kishorsingh and Shamsingh; King Giridhar and the handsome Manohar; Pradyumna, Aniruddha and Purushottam, the fifth; with Govardhan the sixth, born of the Gaud line — kshatriyas who destroyed the clans of enemies, best of kings; the grandson of Gaud Vitthaldas, like the seven-horsed sun; the victorious King Rajsingh, son of Arjun; the hero Viramdev and Ramsingh of good vows, and Raysingh likewise — these three of the Shishodia line; the glorious King Amarsingh of the Chandravat line; Arindam, the general of the lord of Chandrapur; Dvarkaji, Jivaji, Parsoji and Balaji, and Tryambak, son of King Sharif, eager for battle — all these Bhosles, very strong and valiant; Surji Gaikwad and the strong-armed Yesaji; the famous King Dinkar of the Kakade line; three of the Khandagale line named Tryambak, Anant and Datta; the two kings Datta and Rustam of the Yadava line; the hero Rambha Pawar, son of Sarvajit; the wife of Udayram, mother of Jagjivan, whom they called Raybagin, "the royal tigress", as if she were a tigress in battle — she, alas, of great valour, her weapons never checked, with her own brave kinsmen, Krishnaraj, Prachand and the rest; Ghatge Sarjerao, Kamal Gadhe, and Jasvant Kokate with Kamal — all these strong-armed men, at the command of the lord of Delhi, each with his own army, followed the general Shaista Khan.

56. Those forces too drained the rivers all along their way, so that the rivers could not meet their husband, the ocean, until the rains came.

57–59. Then Shaista Khan, of firm resolve, ruler of armies, surrounded by seven times seven thousand horses, by elephants of the noble breed, deep-voiced and like mountains, frightening the elephants of the quarters, and by foot soldiers with fast-running muskets, armed and with every kind of means of war, reached the river Bhima, the boundary of the enemy's land.

60–62. Then that land, with its temples of the gods torn down, its monasteries and mosques broken to pieces, the houses of its officials being demolished, its gardens' great trees broken, many villages and towns emptied of people, its river banks laid bare by the Muslim army moving everywhere, looked like the disc of the moon swallowed whole by Rahu — then less fair to see.

63. Then that army, coming swiftly like the stirred-up ocean, threw the country of Chakravati (Chakan) into alarm.

64. While the great King Shiva was on the hill of Pranala, the red-faced Mughals angrily laid siege to the city of Chakravati.

65. There Shiva's foot soldiers inside the fort, skilled in war, fought for many days.

66. "As long as the angry king fights the strong Johar on the hill of Pranala, we, all united, will fight the enemy step by step in the Chakravati country." Even the ruler of armies did not think lightly of this resolve of the men in the fort.

67. Ali Shah too, learning that the army of the lord of Delhi, of famous and vast strength, was fighting there at the fort, felt some comfort in his heart. Proudly, wishing for victory, he wrote loudly to Johar from the city: "Besiege this hostile man by force on the hill of Pranala, and take him."

*Thus ends the twenty-fifth canto, called "The Coming of Shaista Khan", in the Samhita of a hundred thousand verses, the Anupurana of the line of the Sun, brought to light by Kavindra Paramananda of Nidhivasa.*
